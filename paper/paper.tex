\title{A Four-Core Zero-BRAM FPGA Accelerator for Implied-Volatility Solving and Concurrent Greek Evaluation}

\author{
\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Affiliation Withheld for Double-Blind Review}}
}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Streaming option analytics require repeated implied-volatility (IV) solving and risk sensitivity evaluation under low-latency, tight resource constraints. We present a four-core FPGA accelerator for Black-Scholes IV solving with concurrent Delta, Vega, and Gamma extraction. The design integrates a Brenner-Subrahmanyam analytical seed, a fully pipelined fixed-point Black-Scholes datapath, and distributed-LUTRAM/SRL context storage with no Block RAM or UltraRAM in the accelerator core. Implemented on AMD Artix-7 200T and Alveo U50, the configurations achieve nominal post-route timing closure at 100~MHz and 250~MHz, delivering nominal peak throughputs of 400~MOps/s and 1.00~GOps/s, corresponding to a nominal pass-adjusted datapath service capacity of 151.82~MOps/s on Artix-7 (derived from nominal peak pass capacity and measured mean pass count; not a closed-loop measurement) and 379.57~MOps/s on U50 in the synthetic liquid-moneyness regime ($\bar{P}=2.635$ passes; $3.04\times$ faster than fixed 8-pass in algorithmic ablation at comparable modeled accuracy). In full-system co-simulation under a 64-credit array window (16 credits/core), the canonical workload sustains 8.14~MOps/s closed-loop RTL testbench throughput with zero loss (8.18~MOps/s in saturated memory ablation). 88.7\% of liquid-moneyness options converge in $\le 3$ passes. For the synthetic liquid-moneyness subset ($0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$, $N=4{,}428$), converged Greek MAEs are $4.41{\times}10^{-3}$ for Delta, 0.0971 for Vega, and $2.57{\times}10^{-4}$ for Gamma (liquid-moneyness IV MAE: 7.67~vol-bps, median 0.91~vol-bps). Post-route power estimates are 4.258~W on Artix-7 and 9.560~W on U50 (10.65 and 9.56~nJ/pass peak ingress proxy; 28.05 and 25.19~nJ/contract nominal liquid). Replacing 28 RAMB18E1 primitives with distributed LUTRAM/SRL storage (+857 LUTs, $+0.81\%$) achieves timing closure at 100~MHz (+0.109~ns WNS delta) while leaving all 365 BRAM36 blocks available for system buffering. Replay on 1,382 CBOE SPX quotes validates dynamic-range normalization up to \$7,643 spot with zero arithmetic overflow observed. The architecture delivers cycle-deterministic per-pass execution (cold transaction latency is 230 AXI cycles / 227 core cycles; total latency is pass-dependent); PHY, MAC, and PCIe transfers remain outside the measurement boundary.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{figure*}[t]

\caption{Microarchitecture of the 4-Core Pipelined Implied Volatility and Greeks Engine, illustrating dynamic-range normalization, Brenner-Subrahmanyam analytical guess generator with shared $\sqrt{T}$ path, pipelined datapath, Zero-BRAM distributed context store, and 128-bit egress bus.}
\label{fig:arch}

The fundamental architectural challenge in pipelining variable-iteration numerical solvers on spatial FPGA fabrics is sustaining an Initiation Interval of $II=1$ without replicating the datapath across iterations~\cite{omahony2024role}. Fully unrolling scales DSP and logic resources linearly with maximum pass count, exhausting device capacity; conversely, naive iterative cores stall ingress during downstream convergence. Sustaining $II=1$ feedforward throughput under recirculation requires solving three coupled problems: (1) preserving per-transaction context across variable recirculation paths without consuming Block RAM needed for system buffering, (2) preventing circular deadlock and queue collapse when loopback traffic preempts streaming ingress, and (3) executing transcendentals and division with deterministic per-pass latency so arbitration never stalls the datapath. Black-Scholes implied-volatility (IV) inversion represents a demanding real-world driver for this execution model~\cite{black1973pricing}: each pass evaluates transcendentals (logarithm, exponential, Gaussian CDF) and non-restoring division, while contracts exhibit heterogeneous convergence across market moneyness regimes.

To overcome these challenges, we present a four-core, zero-Block-RAM FPGA accelerator for streaming IV solving and concurrent Greek evaluation. The design achieves $II=1$ feedforward pipelining across three throughput tiers: peak datapath ingress of 400.00~MOps/s on Artix-7 (1,000.00~MOps/s on Alveo U50), open-loop nominal pass-adjusted datapath service capacity of 151.82~MOps/s for synthetic liquid-moneyness regimes ($\bar{P}=2.635$ passes; derived from nominal peak pass capacity and measured mean pass count; not a closed-loop sustained-throughput measurement), and measured 64-credit closed-loop RTL testbench throughput of 8.14~MOps/s on the canonical suite under a 16-credit/core window (9.16~MOps/s theoretical Little's Law bound; 8.18~MOps/s in saturated memory ablation).

\subsection{Key Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Microarchitecture (Zero-BRAM \& Priority Loopback):} We design a variable-iteration spatial microarchitecture executing at $II=1$ ingress with zero Block RAM or UltraRAM in the accelerator core. Replacing 28 RAMB18E1 primitives with distributed LUTRAM/SRL32s (+857 LUTs, $+0.81\%$) provides placement and routing flexibility that secures a $+0.109$~ns WNS advantage, enabling timing closure at the 100~MHz target (derived $F_{\max} = 100.05$~MHz). A strict 4-slot scoreboard headroom reservation ($N_{\text{active}} \le 60$) and loopback-priority arbiter preserve forward progress under the stated arbitration invariant; continuous burst injections up to 256 contracts show zero observed deadlocks.
    \item \textbf{Numerical \& Hardware Co-Design:} We integrate Brenner-Subrahmanyam analytical guess seeding (reducing synthetic liquid-moneyness passes to $\bar{P}=2.635$, delivering a $3.04\times$ speedup over fixed 8-pass in algorithmic ablation at comparable modeled accuracy) with scale-invariant Q8.24 normalization mapping multi-thousand-dollar quotes (empirically validated on CBOE SPX quotes up to \$7,643 spot with zero arithmetic overflow observed as an out-of-sample physical stress test). A controlled datapath error-source ablation across 10,000 contracts isolates the Horner normal-CDF approximation and associated $t$-division path as the dominant error contributor in the evaluated model (a 70.8\% reduction in liquid-moneyness MAE relative to the bit-accurate emulation baseline, $5.57 \to 1.63$~vol-bps; suite-wide error in the emulation model drops from 155.14 to 11.61~vol-bps).
    \item \textbf{Dual-Platform Physical Implementation \& Validation:} We achieve post-route signoff across 28nm Artix-7 200T (100.00~MHz, 4.258~W, 10.65~nJ/pass peak proxy) and 16nm Alveo U50 (250.00~MHz, 9.560~W, 9.56~nJ/pass) with 0 BRAM in the core. Validated via end-to-end DPI-C co-simulation across 113,882 transactions, confirming 10,000/10,000 exact bit identity against a BRAM baseline, characterizing 1/2/4-core interconnect routing dominance (growing from 37.5\% to 61.4\%), and achieving 7.67~vol-bps liquid-moneyness IV MAE (0.91~vol-bps median) with concurrent Delta, Vega, and Gamma extraction.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mathematical Formulation}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Black-Scholes Model and Option Greeks}
We evaluate European call options under standard Black-Scholes assumptions~\cite{black1973pricing}: continuous compounding at risk-free rate $r$, zero dividend yield ($q=0$), and constant volatility during contract inversion. For spot price $S$, strike $K$, and maturity $T$, the Black-Scholes price $C_{\text{BS}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{BS}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma) = S N(d_1) - K e^{-rT} N(d_2)
\end{equation}
where $N(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^{x} e^{-u^2/2} du$ is the cumulative standard normal distribution, and:
\begin{equation}
d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + \left(r + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)T}{\sigma \sqrt{T}}, \quad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma \sqrt{T}
\end{equation}
Note that $d_2$ is computed strictly as a subtraction $d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T}$, directly reusing intermediate $d_1$ without duplicating the 33-cycle divider.

The primary Greeks ($\Delta = \partial C/\partial S$, $\nu = \partial C/\partial \sigma$, $\Gamma = \partial^2 C/\partial S^2$) evaluate concurrently as:
\begin{equation}
\Delta = N(d_1), \quad \nu = S \sqrt{T} N'(d_1), \quad \Gamma = \frac{N'(d_1)}{S \sigma \sqrt{T}}
\end{equation}
where $N'(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-x^2/2}$ is the standard normal PDF.

\subsection{Dynamic-Range Normalization}
The engine operates in native Q8.24 fixed-point dollars ($S, K \le \$128.00$). Native prices ($S, K, C_{\text{mkt}}$) use unsigned Q8.24 ($[0, 255.999]$), allowing sum $(S+K)$ to evaluate up to $\$256$ in an internal 9-bit field (Q9.24) inside the adder before normalizing, while deviates and sensitivities ($d_1, d_2, \Delta, \sigma$) use signed Q8.24 with saturation logic. Transcendental inputs are scale-invariant: the Pad\'{e} ratio $(S - K)/(S + K) \equiv (S/K - 1)/(S/K + 1)$ is identical in dollars or normalized units, eliminating an ingress divider. Deviates $d_1, d_2$, discount $e^{-rT}$, and probabilities $N(d_1), N(d_2)$ are dimensionless. Thus, $C_{\text{BS}}$ and $\nu$ evaluate directly in dollars, checking $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| \le \$0.0100$ without denormalization penalty.

For high-notional index contracts (e.g., SPX at $\$5{,}000$), the architecture exploits Black-Scholes linear homogeneity: $C_{\text{BS}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma) = K \cdot C_{\text{BS}}(S/K, 1.0, T, r, \sigma)$. Pre-normalized inputs $(\tilde{S} = S/K, \tilde{K} = 1.0, \tilde{C}_{\text{mkt}} = C_{\text{mkt}}/K)$ enter directly with threshold $\tilde{\epsilon} = \$0.01 / K$, mapping high-notional quotes into compact Q8.24 without datapath widening. Because price residual and Vega scale proportionally under normalization ($\Delta\tilde{C} / \tilde{\nu} = (\Delta C / K) / (\nu / K) = \Delta C / \nu$), the Newton-Raphson update step $\Delta\sigma$ preserves scale-invariant proportionality. Empirical replay demonstrates successful Q8.24 normalized evaluation on \$7,500+ SPX quotes with zero arithmetic overflow observed. Throughout this paper, error is reported in volatility basis points: $1\text{ vol-bps} = 10^{-4}$ ($0.01\%$) in absolute volatility ($|\sigma_{\text{calc}} - \sigma_{\text{ref}}|$). We quantify moneyness as $m = S/K$ (reciprocal $K/S = 1/m$). Synthetic evaluation vectors sample $K/S \in [0.70, 1.40]$ ($m \in [0.714, 1.429]$); synthetic liquid-moneyness denotes $m \in [0.85, 1.15]$ ($K/S \in [0.87, 1.18]$), deep wings denote $m < 0.85$ or $m > 1.15$, and near-the-money (NTM) denotes $K/S \in [0.90, 1.10]$ ($m \in [0.909, 1.111]$).

\textit{Word-Length Precision Exploration:} We conduct a systematic word-length Pareto exploration across 24-bit to 48-bit fixed-point formats and 32-bit floating-point (Section~\ref{sec:eval_precision}, Fig.~\ref{fig:precision_pareto}). Across 10,000 contracts, Q8.24 emerges as the selected design point: narrower formats (Q6.18, Q7.21) suffer catastrophic dynamic-range or polynomial truncation ($>1{,}000$~vol-bps MAE), while wider formats (Q9.27, Q12.36) and FP32 exceed Artix-7 200T DSP capacity ($>740$ DSPs, up to 155.7\% utilization) without accuracy gains under exchange stopping criteria.

\subsection{Analytical Guess Seeding (Brenner-Subrahmanyam)}
Standard Newton-Raphson update steps follow $\sigma_{n+1} = \sigma_n - [C_{\text{BS}}(\sigma_n) - C_{\text{mkt}}]/\nu(\sigma_n)$. Unseeded guesses (e.g., $\sigma_0 = 0.20$) require more iterations and can diverge when $\nu \to 0$. In contrast, the Brenner-Subrahmanyam approximation~\cite{brenner1988simple} requires only a scalar multiply and non-restoring divider while directly reusing the shared $\sqrt{T}$ unit. Alternatives like Corrado-Miller~\cite{corrado1996improved} require an extra square-root unit ($>30$ cycles), whereas rational expansions like J{\"a}ckel~\cite{jackel2015lets} consume dozens of DSPs. Replacing spot $S$ with symmetric mean $(S+K)/2$ prevents near-the-money bias:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_0 = \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\, C_{\text{mkt}}}{\sqrt{T} \cdot (S + K)/2} \approx \frac{2.5066\, C_{\text{mkt}}}{\sqrt{T} \cdot (S + K)/2}
\end{equation}
Because both $C_{\text{mkt}}$ and $(S+K)/2$ scale linearly with $K$, this ratio is scale-invariant under normalization. Note that $\sigma_0 \propto 1/\sqrt{T}$, evaluated directly with $\sqrt{T}$ in the denominator of the 33-cycle guess divider. In hardware (64 cycles), this closed-form seed provides an accurate initial point: across the synthetic liquid-moneyness regime, relative seed error remains below 0.5\%, enabling 88.7\% of liquid-moneyness contracts to converge in $\le 3$ passes (averaging $\bar{P} = 2.635$ passes per contract, and 3.651 across the extended domain).

\subsection{High-Speed Polynomial and Elementary Function Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pad\'{e} Logarithm:} We evaluate the [1/1] Pad\'{e} approximant $\ln_{[1/1]}(x) = 2 \frac{x - 1}{x + 1}$ for $x = S/K$ (from $\ln x = 2\operatorname{atanh}(\frac{x-1}{x+1})$). The analytical error $\max_{x \in [0.85, 1.15]} |\ln(x) - \ln_{[1/1]}(x)| \le 3.6 \times 10^{-4}$ ($<0.22\%$ relative) is obtained by maximizing the closed-form remainder $R(u) \approx \frac{2}{3}u^3$ for $u = \frac{x-1}{x+1} \le 0.070$ in real arithmetic prior to quantization. For deep wings ($|S-K| > 0.15K$), the engine engages an 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm fallback (SRL15-padded to 33 cycles), preserving fixed datapath latency.
    \item \textbf{Horner Normal CDF:} $N(x)$ follows Abramowitz \& Stegun (7.1.26)~\cite{abramowitz1964handbook}: $N(x) = 1 - Z(x)\sum_{i=1}^5 b_i t^i$, where scale factor $t = 1/(1+p|x|)$ is evaluated by a dedicated 33-cycle non-restoring divider ($t_1, t_2$ for $d_1, d_2$), followed by 5th-order Horner single-DSP MAC stages. The theoretical error $|\epsilon| \le 7.5 \times 10^{-8}$ ($x \ge 0$) refers to the real-valued formulation before coefficient quantization; complete fixed-point error is evaluated in end-to-end DPI-C co-simulation. The pipeline applies symmetry $N(-x) = 1 - N(x)$, clamping to $[0, 1]$ with standard coefficients ($p=0.2316419$, $b_1 \dots b_5$~\cite{abramowitz1964handbook}).
    \item \textbf{Hyperbolic CORDIC:} Exponentials $e^{-rT}$ and $e^{-d_1^2/2}$ are computed using a fully pipelined 16-stage hyperbolic CORDIC engine.
\end{itemize}

\section{Hardware Microarchitecture}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
The proposed engine implements a \textit{Variable-Iteration Feedback Execution Pattern} for deeply pipelined spatial datapaths (Fig.~\ref{fig:arch}). The architecture coordinates seven concrete hardware mechanisms: (1) an explicit 6-bit Transaction ID (TID) tracking up to 64 in-flight contracts per core, (2) persistent invariant parameter context stored in distributed LUTRAM, eliminating on-chip BRAM allocation, (3) a single shared $II=1$ feedforward datapath reused across passes without spatial replication, (4) a cycle-deterministic per-pass latency (159 cycles) that eliminates pipeline arbitration stalls, (5) a convergence decision block routing completed transactions to egress and non-converged transactions to loopback, (6) strict loopback priority arbitration ensuring recirculating contracts never experience pipeline starvation, and (7) a scoreboard-driven 4-slot headroom reservation ($N_{\text{active}} \le 60$) designed to preserve forward progress under the stated arbitration invariant and prevent circular waiting by reserving four slots for loopback traffic.

\subsection{Stage 1: Ingress Registration \& Queuing (1 Cycle)}
The ingress interface receives a 256-bit AXI4-Stream packet containing $\{S, K, T, r, C_{\text{mkt}}, \text{TID}\}$. Ingress values are registered in 1 clock cycle and tagged with a 6-bit TID. Normalization division is omitted; the Pad\'{e} ratio $\frac{S-K}{S+K} \equiv \frac{\tilde{S}-1}{\tilde{S}+1}$ is evaluated inside Stage 3, saving 32 cycles.

\subsection{Stage 2: Analytical Guess Seeder \& Shared Sqrt Unit (64 Cycles)}
The seeder evaluates Eq.~(4): $\sigma_0 = \frac{2.5066\, C_{\text{mkt}}}{\sqrt{T} \cdot (S + K)/2}$. A digit-recurrence unit computes $\sqrt{T}$ in 29 cycles (plus alignment), storing $\sqrt{T}$ in distributed context RAM and forwarding it downstream, eliminating a redundant square-root core. An internal non-restoring divider evaluates the ratio in 33 cycles, producing a valid $\sigma_0$ in exactly 64 cycles.

\subsection{Stage 3: Core Pipelined Black-Scholes Datapath (126 Cycles)}
The core Stage-3 datapath is a 126-cycle fully unrolled feedforward pipeline measured from input register to output register (including all delay-matching SRLs and CORDIC padding, verified in cycle-accurate RTL simulation), accepting one contract/cycle ($II = 1$). Sub-operations are partitioned across registered boundaries. For deep wings ($|S-K| > 0.15K$), the 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm is latency-matched via SRL15 to 33 cycles, maintaining a fixed 126-cycle latency for each Stage-3 pass.

\subsection{Zero-BRAM Context Memory Architecture}
Conventional designs map FIFOs to Block RAMs (RAMB18/36). Our engine implements all context stores ($64 \times 32$-bit) exclusively via distributed LUTRAM (\texttt{ram\_style = "distributed"}) and SRL32s, consuming 0 BRAMs/URAMs in the accelerator core. Feedforward state flows through SRL32s ($II=1$), while distributed stores retain static parameters $\{S, K, C_{\text{mkt}}, r, T\}$ across transaction lifetimes (227 core cycles cold / 230 AXI cycles, 386 loopback) to reinject data for NR updates. Each core provisions 64 physical context slots (256 slots total), tracked by \texttt{in\_flight\_count} and a 64-bit scoreboard (\texttt{tid\_busy\_mask}) asserting backpressure when in-flight contracts reach $\ge 60$ slots ($\ge 4$ headroom slots) or the requested local TID is active. Under this policy, the four-core array admits up to $4 \times 60 = 240$ active in-flight transactions simultaneously. Because loopback retains its pre-allocated slot with strict priority over new ingress, re-entry cannot be blocked, preventing deadlock. On Artix-7, this consumes 1,256 LUTRAM and 1,804 SRL32s within context stores (314 LUTRAM, 451 SRL32 per core), with 6,852 SRLs across the datapath for delay matching (8,656 total SRLs, 18.74\%); on Alveo U50, it occupies 0.28\% of LUTs, leaving all BRAM/URAM blocks unconstrained for external system buffering (PHY/MAC/PCIe outside boundary).

\textit{Controlled BRAM Ablation:} A matched Artix-7 baseline replacing the distributed context store with synchronous BRAM produced bit-exact IV/Greek outputs for all 10,000 canonical contracts (10,000/10,000 exact bit identity). The BRAM baseline consumed 28 RAMB18 blocks and incurred one additional cycle of AXI cold latency (231 vs. 230 cycles; 699.55 vs. 695.67 cycles mean latency, Section~\ref{sec:bram_ablation}).

\subsection{Stage 4: Newton-Raphson Update Step \& Greek Dividers (33 Cycles)}
Following Stage 3, each core checks convergence $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| \le \epsilon$, where $\epsilon = 32'd167772 \approx \$0.0100$ in Q8.24 (CBOE MPV~\cite{cboe2023specs}). By first-order Taylor expansion, implied-volatility error satisfies $|\Delta\sigma| \approx |\Delta C| / \nu$. For near-the-money contracts in the evaluated moderate-maturity regime ($T \ge 0.10$~yr), Vega is typically large; consequently, the measured \$0.01 price residual corresponds to small volatility errors in the NTM benchmark (IV MAE = 1.57~vol-bps). In boundary regimes where Vega vanishes ($\nu \to 0$, such as deep wings or short expiries $T < 14$~days), the same price residual can correspond to substantially larger volatility differences. Price-domain convergence provides a fixed market-price residual criterion while preventing unproductive solver iterations in low-Vega wings. Stage-4 latency (33 cycles) is measured from Stage-3 output to the loopback/egress register. A contract is classified as converged strictly when satisfying $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| \le \epsilon$; reaching the 8-pass ceiling without meeting this residual registers as non-convergence failure. If $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| > \epsilon$, update $\Delta\sigma = (C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}})/\nu$ evaluates via a 33-cycle divider (\texttt{u\_nr\_divider}) concurrently with the 33-cycle Gamma divider (\texttt{u\_gamma\_divider}). Hardware clamping prevents oscillation as $\nu \to 0$: NR step bounded by $|\Delta\sigma| \le 0.25$ (\texttt{MAX\_STEP\_Q24}), volatility clamped to $[0.01, 5.00]$, seed clamped to $[0.05, 3.00]$, and \texttt{u\_nr\_divider} saturates upon zero denominator. Refined $\sigma_{n+1}$ loops back with priority via the active TID scoreboard. Because Greeks register to egress solely upon convergence, multi-pass contracts re-evaluate $\Delta, \nu, \Gamma$ at $\sigma_{n+1}$, ensuring exported sensitivities match final converged IV.

\subsection{Stage 5: Multi-Core Arbitration \& 128-Bit Egress (2 Cycles)}
Upon convergence, results are packed into a 128-bit data payload: $\text{Egress}[127:0] = \{ \text{TID}[5:0],\, \Gamma[25:0],\, \nu[31:0],\, \Delta[31:0],\, \sigma[31:0] \}$, carrying the 6-bit local TID, 26-bit Gamma, 32-bit Vega, 32-bit Delta, and 32-bit IV ($6+26+32+32+32=128$ bits). The 2-bit originating core ID (\texttt{active\_rd[1:0]} in \texttt{iv\_multi\_engine\_top.sv}) is retained at egress as an interface sideband/metadata signal. The array-wide composite global tag is $\{\text{Core}[1:0], \text{TID}[5:0]\}$ (8 bits), uniquely distinguishing up to 240 admitted in-flight transactions across the 256 provisioned slots without a reorder buffer. Egress words are emitted in work-conserving arbitration order. Each core enforces local TID uniqueness across its 64 slots via \texttt{tid\_busy\_mask[63:0]}. $\Gamma$ occupies 26 bits (signed Q2.24, $[-2.0, +1.999]$) by truncating 6 redundant sign bits from Q8.24; while bounded by $\Gamma \le 1/(S\sigma\sqrt{2\pi T})$, this packing is empirically confirmed without saturation across all 113,882 evaluated transactions in the full-system validation suite (Datasets A--D, max: 0.2357; formal range safety across all theoretically conceivable $\{S,T,\sigma\}$ is not claimed). Extreme inputs are protected by hardware clamping at the divider output to prevent arithmetic wraparound. A 4-way arbiter drains output FIFOs in 2 cycles.

\subsection{Pipeline Latency and Sustained Throughput Reconciliation}
We distinguish four terms: a \textbf{contract} denotes one input quote; a \textbf{pass} denotes one evaluation through Stage-3/Stage-4; an \textbf{iteration} denotes an unseeded Newton-Raphson cycle; and an \textbf{operation (Op)} denotes one converged contract producing final IV and all three Greeks ($\Delta, \nu, \Gamma$). Stage-3 $II=1$ describes feedforward acceptance, while sustained throughput reflects completed contracts adjusted by average passes. Internal cold latency is 227 cycles: 1 cycle Stage 1, 64 cycles Stage 2, 1 queue cycle, 126 cycles Stage 3, 33 cycles Stage 4, and 2 cycles Stage 5 (2.27~$\mu$s on Artix-7 at 100~MHz / 0.908~$\mu$s on U50 at 250~MHz; isolated Stage 3 is 126 cycles, 1.26~$\mu$s / 0.504~$\mu$s). Including AXI handshake overhead, end-to-end cold latency is 230 cycles on Zero-BRAM (2.30~$\mu$s on Artix-7 / 0.920~$\mu$s on U50; 231 cycles on BRAM baseline). A loopback pass adds 159 cycles, yielding 389 cycles for a two-pass transaction (386 core cycles). Loopback does not stall the Stage-3 $II=1$ datapath; each loopback consumes one ingress slot with strict priority over incoming traffic. Pass-adjusted nominal datapath service capacity is derived from $II=1$ peak ingress divided by empirical average passes ($T_{\text{nominal}} = T_{\text{peak}} / \bar{P}$); it represents open-loop datapath service capacity and is distinct from closed-loop credit-constrained RTL co-simulation throughput (8.14~MOps/s on the canonical suite under a 64-credit window [16 credits/core], Table~\ref{tab:saturation_diagnostics}; 8.18~MOps/s in saturated memory ablation). By Little's Law ($T = N / L$), with 64 outstanding credits across 4 cores and 698.4-cycle mean round-trip latency ($6.984\,\mu\text{s}$ at 100~MHz), the testbench is concurrency-limited to $64 / 6.984\,\mu\text{s} \approx 9.16$~MOps/s. We report 8.14~MOps/s as a closed-loop credit-constrained RTL verification metric, not as the physical saturation rate of the datapath. On Alveo U50, pass-adjusted nominal rates (379.57~MOps/s liquid-moneyness) reflect projected nominal capacity at 250~MHz, not physical co-simulation.

\textbf{Synthetic Liquid-Moneyness Regime ($m \in [0.85, 1.15]$):} Across 4,428 contracts in the synthetic liquid-moneyness subset under bit-accurate Q8.24 evaluation, 88.7\% converge within $\le 3$ passes (Pass 2: 49.5\%, Pass 3: 38.8\%, Pass 4: 10.1\%, Pass 5--8: 1.2\%), with an average of $\bar{P} = 2.635$ passes per contract. Across 4 cores at 100~MHz, nominal pass-adjusted datapath service capacity is $T_{\text{nominal, liquid}} = \frac{400.00\text{ MOps/s}}{2.635} = \mathbf{151.82\text{ MOps/s}}$ (379.57~MOps/s on Alveo U50), achieving 7.67~vol-bps liquid-moneyness IV MAE in synthesized RTL co-simulation (4.80~vol-bps in idealized algorithmic modeling). In the algorithmic ablation, dynamic loopback achieves a $3.04\times$ throughput speedup over fixed 8-pass (50.00~MOps/s, 4.50~vol-bps) at comparable modeled accuracy (within 0.30~vol-bps).

\textbf{Extended-Domain Service Capacity:} Across the entire 10,000-contract extended suite ($0.70 \le S/K \le 1.40$), contracts average $\bar{P} = 3.651$ passes, delivering a nominal pass-adjusted datapath service capacity of \textbf{109.55~MOps/s} on Artix-7 and \textbf{273.88~MOps/s} on Alveo U50. Spatial unrolling at $II=1$ would require 1,216 DSPs (164.3\% of Artix-7 200T) for 2 passes and 2,432 DSPs for 4 passes, confirming that dynamic loopback is the evaluated $II=1$ spatial configuration fitting within device DSP capacity.

\subsection{Zero-BRAM Context Signoff Comparison}
\label{sec:bram_ablation}
To quantify the benefit of the Zero-BRAM microarchitecture, we implemented an identical four-core baseline using dedicated Block RAM primitives (28 RAMB18E1, equivalent to 14 BRAM36 blocks) for context storage. \subsection{Numerical Accuracy and Greeks Extraction}
\noindent\textbf{Experimental Methodology Taxonomy:} To ensure methodological transparency, empirical evaluations are categorized into four evidentiary tiers: (1)~\textit{Post-Route Physical Implementation}: placed-and-routed timing, utilization, and power models from AMD Vivado 2025.2 on Artix-7 200T and Alveo U50; (2)~\textit{Closed-Loop Synthesized RTL Co-Simulation}: cycle-accurate DPI-C execution of synthesized RTL under closed-loop credit flow control (measuring 8.14~MOps/s and verifying bit-level identity); (3)~\textit{Open-Loop Nominal Datapath Service Capacity}: analytical service rate from $II=1$ ingress rate divided by measured pass counts ($T_{\text{nominal}} = T_{\text{peak}} / \bar{P} = 151.82$~MOps/s); and (4)~\textit{Algorithmic and Architectural Ablation Models}: bit-accurate software models isolating arithmetic truncation, fixed-iteration baselines, and alternative wordlengths.

Over the extended domain ($K/S \in [0.70, 1.40]$), Table~\ref{tab:greek_accuracy} details the simultaneous accuracy of IV and all three concurrent Greeks extracted from the 128-bit egress bus for the finalized 10,000-contract synthesized RTL co-simulation (Dataset A): in the synthetic liquid-moneyness subset ($N=4{,}428$), IV MAE is $7.67 \times 10^{-4}$ (7.67~vol-bps; median $0.91$~vol-bps), Delta MAE is $4.41 \times 10^{-3}$ (median $1.80 \times 10^{-4}$), Vega MAE is 0.0971 (median $1.64 \times 10^{-3}$), and Gamma MAE is $2.57 \times 10^{-4}$ (median $1.20 \times 10^{-5}$). Furthermore, 48-bit Q12.36 yields an identical liquid-moneyness MAE of 5.70~vol-bps (pre-update) to Q8.24 because analytical truncation errors in the [1/1] Pad\'{e} logarithm and 5th-order Horner polynomial dominate machine epsilon ($2^{-36} \approx 1.45 \times 10^{-11}$). Q8.24 thus represents the selected accuracy/resource operating point satisfying both physical DSP constraints and numerical convergence requirements on the target Artix-7 device (with non-Q8.24 DSP counts estimated via DSP48E1 multiplier decomposition).

To evaluate robustness under extreme trading regimes, Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary_stress} examines a 2,500-point 2D boundary surface alongside offline market-data replay.

\textit{Boundary Stress Surface (Part 6A):} In Fig.~\ref{fig:boundary_stress}(a), moneyness $S/K \in [0.70, 1.40]$ and maturity $T \in [0.0027, 2.0]$~yr are sampled on a dense $50 \times 50$ grid. The stress surface identifies low-Vega and approximation-transition regions as numerical boundary regimes requiring additional iterations and exhibiting substantially larger IV error: (1)~\textit{Low-Vega Boundary} ($T < 14$~days, 0.0384~yr): as $T \to 0$, Vega vanishes ($\nu \to 0$), causing unconstrained Newton-Raphson updates to destabilize; here, hardware clamping ($|\Delta\sigma| \le 0.25$) successfully bounds the solver step, maintaining finite error (MAE 4,389~vol-bps) without pipeline divergence; (2)~\textit{Pad\'{e} Transition Zone} ($|S/K - 1| \approx 0.15$): switching between the [1/1] Pad\'{e} approximant and CORDIC logarithm introduces a minor boundary step (MAE 415~vol-bps, 4.96 passes). In contrast, within the defined liquid-moneyness interior ($0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15, T \ge 14\text{d}$), the boundary-surface evaluation remains below 5~vol-bps MAE, whereas the full synthesized RTL benchmark over the broader liquid-moneyness distribution reports 7.67~vol-bps MAE.

\textit{Empirical SPX Market-Data Replay (Part 6B):} Offline replay of 1,382 recorded CBOE European SPX call quotes (spot up to $\$7{,}643.99$, across 15 expirations) serves as an out-of-sample physical stress test for dynamic-range normalization and arithmetic overflow immunity. Because live quotes reflect quote discretization and market microstructure dynamics rather than ideal Black-Scholes prices, SPX replay validates operational robustness rather than an algorithmic ground-truth pricing model. Input pre-normalization ($\tilde{S} = S/K, \tilde{K} = 1.0$) mapped multi-thousand-dollar quotes into compact Q8.24 ($\tilde{S} \le 1.3711$) with zero arithmetic overflow observed across all 1,382 quotes, demonstrating successful scale-normalized evaluation without datapath widening. Under our moneyness classification, 1,175 quotes were NTM ($0.95 \le S/K \le 1.05$), 1,367 were in the liquid-moneyness band ($0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$), and 15 were deep-wing. For NTM quotes, \textbf{72.1\% achieved single-pass convergence} (230 cycles, averaging 1.40 passes), with 437.93~vol-bps MAE (78.88~vol-bps median; liquid-moneyness MAE 487.62~vol-bps, 122.84~vol-bps median). The higher average error relative to synthetic Dataset A is associated with low-Vega, near-expiry contracts, where market quote discretization amplifies the volatility error induced by the fixed \$0.01 price threshold ($\Delta\sigma \approx \Delta C / \nu$). The deep-wing subset ($N=15$, MAE 331.52~vol-bps) separately exhibits bounded solver behavior under the hardware step clamp ($|\Delta\sigma| \le 0.25$), precluding arithmetic divergence.

\subsection{End-to-End Four-Core RTL Validation \& System Saturation}
To bridge physical FPGA implementation with algorithmic correctness under streaming traffic, the complete four-core RTL array was validated in an end-to-end DPI-C co-simulation suite across five structured phases:
(1)~\textbf{Diverse Dataset Validation (Phase 7A):} As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:rtl_validation}, the synthesized four-core RTL was evaluated across four workloads totaling 113,882 transactions: Canonical ($N=10\text{k}$), Randomized Stability ($N=100\text{k}$), Boundary Stress Grid ($N=2.5\text{k}$), and CBOE SPX quotes ($N=1{,}382$). Across all 113,882 transactions, strict transaction integrity is maintained with zero lost contracts ($N_{\text{lost}}=0$), zero duplicate egress packets ($N_{\text{dup}}=0$), and zero TID routing mismatches, confirming that dynamic arbitration and distributed context stores preserve strict transaction-to-core mapping. In Dataset A, liquid-moneyness IV MAE is 7.67~vol-bps (median 0.91~vol-bps; NTM MAE 2.01~vol-bps, median 0.96~vol-bps), reconciling with algorithmic models (4.80/5.70~vol-bps) due to 33-cycle integer divider and 16-stage CORDIC quantization in the full RTL model. On SPX quotes (Dataset D), moneyness decomposition confirms zero arithmetic overflow and 72.1\% single-pass convergence for NTM quotes (437.93~vol-bps MAE, 78.88~vol-bps median; 487.62~vol-bps liquid-moneyness MAE, Table~\ref{tab:rtl_validation}).
(2)~\textbf{Randomized Long-Run Stability (Phase 7B):} Continuous injection of 100,000 contracts with randomized TIDs $\in [0, 63]$ ran for 1,170,136 cycles (11.70~ms at 100~MHz) without stalls or scoreboard corruption, achieving 8.55~MOps/s sustained throughput with 14.84~vol-bps liquid-moneyness IV MAE (1.81~vol-bps median) and Greek MAEs of $\Delta = 0.0112$, $\nu = 0.6359$, $\Gamma = 0.0032$.

Our engine achieves 400~MOps/s peak (151.82~MOps/s nominal liquid-moneyness service capacity) with concurrent Greeks and 0 BRAMs; on U50, 1.00~GOps/s at 0.920~$\mu$s AXI cold latency (0.908~$\mu$s core) provides architectural context relative to the 120~MOps/s reported in~\cite{wang2022fpga}, although the workloads, numerical methods, and output sets differ. While Klaisoongnoen et al.~\cite{klaisoongnoen2024evaluating} evaluate AMD Versal AI Engines (AIE) for compute-dense forward pricing and nested Monte Carlo simulation, our work addresses the orthogonal challenge of streaming inverse implied-volatility solving. Rather than accelerating forward simulations on high-end Versal SoCs, we architect a zero-BRAM variable-iteration feedback pipeline tailored for standard FPGA logic fabric, achieving $II=1$ feedforward execution with persistent per-transaction context on Artix-7 and Alveo platforms.

\section{Conclusion}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
We presented a four-core FPGA accelerator for streaming Black-Scholes IV solving and concurrent Greeks ($\Delta, \nu, \Gamma$). Combining Brenner-Subrahmanyam seeding, Q8.24 normalization, and a zero-BRAM microarchitecture, the engine achieves nominal post-route timing closure at 100~MHz on Artix-7 200T (400~MOps/s peak, 4.258~W) and 250~MHz on Alveo U50 (1.00~GOps/s peak, 9.560~W) with 0 BRAM/URAM in the core. Dynamic loopback converges in 2.635 liquid-moneyness passes, delivering 151.82~MOps/s nominal pass-adjusted service capacity on Artix-7 (379.57~MOps/s on U50; 8.14~MOps/s closed-loop RTL testbench throughput) with 7.67~vol-bps liquid-moneyness IV MAE (0.91~vol-bps median) in synthesized RTL co-simulation. For NTM quotes, the solver achieves $1.57 \times 10^{-4}$ IV MAE (99.9\% within $1.0\%$). Replay on 1,382 CBOE SPX quotes validates normalization up to \$7,643 spot with zero arithmetic overflow observed as an out-of-sample physical stress test. The architecture delivers cycle-deterministic per-pass execution, with a 227-cycle core cold path and a measured 230-cycle AXI cold transaction (0.908/0.920~$\mu$s on U50; 2.27/2.30~$\mu$s on Artix-7; total latency pass-dependent), delivering 10.7$\times$ higher nominal liquid-moneyness capacity and a 112.8$\times$ throughput-to-power proxy ratio relative to a 16-thread AVX2 CPU baseline (vendor TDP envelope comparison excluding host PCIe/PHY power).
