\title{A Four-Core Zero-BRAM FPGA Accelerator for Implied-Volatility Solving and Concurrent Greek Evaluation}

\author{
\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Affiliation Withheld for Double-Blind Review}}
}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Streaming option analytics require repeated implied-volatility (IV) solving and risk sensitivity evaluation under low-latency and tight resource constraints. We present a four-core FPGA accelerator for Black-Scholes IV solving with concurrent Delta, Vega, and Gamma extraction. The design integrates a Brenner-Subrahmanyam analytical seed, a fully pipelined fixed-point Black-Scholes datapath, and distributed-LUTRAM/SRL context storage with no Block RAM or UltraRAM in the accelerator core. Implemented on AMD Artix-7 200T and Alveo U50, the configurations achieve nominal post-route timing closure at 100~MHz and 250~MHz, delivering nominal peak throughputs of 400~MOps/s and 1.00~GOps/s, respectively, and net sustained throughputs of 390.62~MOps/s and 976.56~MOps/s in the evaluated liquid regime, with post-route power estimates of 4.258~W and 9.560~W. In DPI-C co-simulation over 10,000 synthetic contracts, the analytical seed enables 97.6\% one-pass convergence on the evaluated liquid subset. The solver achieves an absolute IV MAE of $1.57{\times}10^{-4}$ (1.57~vol-bps) over the evaluated near-the-money suite ($K/S \in [0.90, 1.10]$, $N=10{,}000$) and $8.80{\times}10^{-4}$ across the liquid subset ($0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$, 4,394 contracts) of the wide domain, with 99.9\% of evaluated near-the-money contracts within 1.0 percentage point of reference IV. The corresponding converged Greek MAEs are $4.38{\times}10^{-3}$ for Delta, 0.0963 for Vega, and $2.39{\times}10^{-4}$ for Gamma. The architecture delivers cycle-deterministic per-pass execution (complete transaction latency is pass-dependent). Timing, resource, and power results are post-route implementation estimates, while numerical accuracy is evaluated via fixed-point DPI-C co-simulation; PHY, MAC, and PCIe transfers remain outside the core measurement boundary.
\end{abstract}

\label{fig:arch}

Iterative root-finding algorithms are notoriously difficult to pipeline on FPGAs~\cite{omahony2024role}: the number of loop iterations is data-dependent, transcendental function units are deeply pipelined (33--64 cycles), and in-flight intermediate results must be preserved across variable-length feedback paths. Black-Scholes implied volatility (IV) inversion is a canonical instance of this class~\cite{black1973pricing}. Given market price $C_\text{mkt}$ and contract parameters $\{S, K, T, r\}$, the IV solver inverts $C_\text{BS}(\sigma) = C_\text{mkt}$ via Newton-Raphson (NR), with each pass requiring evaluation of $\sqrt{T}$, $\ln(S/K)$, the CDF, and a division---all sharing deep pipeline resources across a variable number of feedback iterations. Motivated by streaming market-data and option-analytics workloads where market-making systems track IV and risk sensitivities (Delta $\Delta$, Gamma $\Gamma$, Vega $\nu$) across thousands of strikes and expiries~\cite{cboe2023specs}, conventional NR implementations face two hardware obstacles: (1)~\textit{Numerical sensitivity}---when Vega $\nu \to 0$ (short maturities or deep wings), unseeded iterations overshoot or diverge; and (2)~\textit{Non-deterministic latency}---CPU and GPU runtimes incur OS scheduling and PCIe queuing jitter that prevents cycle-deterministic streaming execution. To overcome these challenges, we present a zero-block-RAM FPGA engine for streaming IV solving with cycle-deterministic per-pass execution and concurrent Greeks extraction.

\subsection{Key Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Analytical Guess Engine:} Brenner-Subrahmanyam seeder is tailored for low relative error in the liquid near-the-money regime, enabling 97.6\% 1-pass convergence while forwarding shared $\sqrt{T}$.
    \item \textbf{Scale-Invariant Normalization:} Exploiting Black-Scholes linear homogeneity ($\tilde{S} = S/K, \tilde{K} = 1.0, \tilde{C} = C/K$) bounds dynamic range strictly within Q8.24, supporting high-notional contracts without multi-precision arithmetic.
    \item \textbf{Zero-BRAM Microarchitecture:} Context buffers map to distributed LUTRAM/SRL32 registers (\texttt{ram\_style = "distributed"}), consuming 0 Block RAMs and 0 UltraRAMs in the accelerator core, leaving dedicated on-chip block RAMs available for external packet, DMA, or network interface buffering.
    \item \textbf{Dual-Platform Implementation \& Timing Closure:} Implemented on Artix-7 200T at 100~MHz (400~MOps/s, 4.258~W post-route power estimate, $WNS=+0.005\text{ ns}$) and Alveo U50 at 250~MHz (1.00~GOps/s, 9.560~W post-route estimate, $WNS=+0.073\text{ ns}$) with 0 BRAM/URAM in the accelerator core. DPI-C co-simulation confirms $1.57 \times 10^{-4}$ near-the-money ($8.80 \times 10^{-4}$ liquid) absolute volatility MAE, yielding a peak throughput of 400~MOps/s (28.1$\times$ the companion AVX2 CPU throughput).
\end{itemize}

\section{Mathematical Formulation}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Black-Scholes Model and Option Greeks}
We evaluate European call options under standard Black-Scholes assumptions~\cite{black1973pricing}: continuous compounding at risk-free rate $r$, zero dividend yield ($q=0$), and constant volatility during contract inversion. For spot price $S$, strike $K$, and maturity $T$, the Black-Scholes price $C_{\text{BS}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{BS}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma) = S N(d_1) - K e^{-rT} N(d_2)
\end{equation}
where $N(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^{x} e^{-u^2/2} du$ is the cumulative standard normal distribution, and:
\begin{equation}
d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + \left(r + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)T}{\sigma \sqrt{T}}, \quad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma \sqrt{T}
\end{equation}
Note that $d_2$ is computed strictly as a subtraction $d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T}$, directly reusing intermediate $d_1$ without duplicating the 33-cycle divider.

The primary Greeks ($\Delta = \partial C/\partial S$, $\nu = \partial C/\partial \sigma$, $\Gamma = \partial^2 C/\partial S^2$) evaluate concurrently as:
\begin{equation}
\Delta = N(d_1), \quad \nu = S \sqrt{T} N'(d_1), \quad \Gamma = \frac{N'(d_1)}{S \sigma \sqrt{T}}
\end{equation}
where $N'(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-x^2/2}$ is the standard normal PDF.

\subsection{Scale-Invariance Normalization}
The engine operates in native Q8.24 fixed-point dollars ($S, K \le \$128.00$). Native prices ($S, K, C_{\text{mkt}}$) use unsigned Q8.24 ($[0, 255.999]$), allowing sum $(S+K)$ to evaluate up to $\$256$ in an internal 9-bit integer field (Q9.24) inside the adder before normalizing, while deviates and sensitivities ($d_1, d_2, \Delta, \sigma$) use signed Q8.24 with saturation logic. Transcendental inputs are scale-invariant: the Pad\'{e} ratio $(S - K)/(S + K) \equiv (S/K - 1)/(S/K + 1)$ is identical in dollars or normalized units, eliminating an ingress divider. Deviates $d_1, d_2$, discount $e^{-rT}$, and probabilities $N(d_1), N(d_2)$ are dimensionless. Thus, $C_{\text{BS}}$ and $\nu$ evaluate directly in dollars, checking $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| \le \$0.0100$ without denormalization penalty.

For high-notional index contracts (e.g., SPX at $\$5{,}000$), we exploit linear homogeneity: $C_{\text{BS}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma) = K \cdot C_{\text{BS}}(S/K, 1.0, T, r, \sigma)$. Pre-normalized inputs $(\tilde{S} = S/K, \tilde{K} = 1.0, \tilde{C}_{\text{mkt}} = C_{\text{mkt}}/K)$ enter the engine directly with threshold $\tilde{\epsilon} = \$0.01 / K$, bounding the principal normalized price variables to a compact Q8.24 range without datapath widening. Because price residual error and Vega scale proportionally under normalization ($\Delta\tilde{C} / \tilde{\nu} = (\Delta C / K) / (\nu / K) = \Delta C / \nu$), the Newton-Raphson update step $\Delta\sigma$ is scale-invariant. We quantify moneyness as $S/K$ (or reciprocally $K/S$), where $0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$ (approximately $0.87 \le K/S \le 1.18$) defines the primary liquid trading regime.

\textit{Word-Length Precision Exploration:} Precision analysis across 5,000 contracts confirms Q8.24 provides the selected accuracy-resource-frequency trade-off among evaluated formats: narrower 24-bit Q6.18 fails (44.6\% volatility MAE) from underflow in near-zero Vega divisions, while wider 48-bit Q12.36 doubles LUT footprint (1,711 to 3,240 LUTs) and lowers $F_{\max}$ from 250 to 185~MHz with zero accuracy gain under the \$0.01 residual. FP32 requires $2.8\times$ higher logic LUT density and 16 DSPs/core with variable latency.

\subsection{Analytical Guess Seeding (Brenner-Subrahmanyam)}
Standard Newton-Raphson update steps follow $\sigma_{n+1} = \sigma_n - [C_{\text{BS}}(\sigma_n) - C_{\text{mkt}}]/\nu(\sigma_n)$. Unseeded guesses (e.g., $\sigma_0 = 0.20$) require more iterations and can diverge when $\nu \to 0$. In contrast, the Brenner-Subrahmanyam approximation~\cite{brenner1988simple} requires only a scalar multiply and non-restoring divider while directly reusing the shared $\sqrt{T}$ unit. Alternatives like Corrado-Miller~\cite{corrado1996improved} require an extra square-root unit ($>30$ cycles), whereas rational expansions like J{\"a}ckel~\cite{jackel2015lets} consume dozens of DSPs. Replacing spot $S$ with symmetric mean $(S+K)/2$ prevents near-the-money bias:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_0 = \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\, C_{\text{mkt}}}{\sqrt{T} \cdot (S + K)/2} \approx \frac{2.5066\, C_{\text{mkt}}}{\sqrt{T} \cdot (S + K)/2}
\end{equation}
Because both $C_{\text{mkt}}$ and $(S+K)/2$ scale linearly with $K$, this ratio is scale-invariant under normalization. Note that $\sigma_0 \propto 1/\sqrt{T}$, evaluated directly with $\sqrt{T}$ in the denominator of the 33-cycle guess divider. In hardware (64 cycles), this closed-form seed provides an accurate starting point: in the evaluated liquid regime, relative seed error was below 0.5\%; in the evaluated wing samples, absolute error was below 0.08~vol, enabling single-pass convergence for 97.6\% of liquid quotes (deep wings average 2.100 passes).

\subsection{High-Speed Polynomial and Elementary Function Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pad\'{e} Logarithm:} We evaluate the [1/1] Pad\'{e} approximant $\ln(x) \approx 2 \frac{x - 1}{x + 1}$ for $x = S/K$ (from $\ln x = 2\operatorname{atanh}(\frac{x-1}{x+1})$). Maximum absolute error $\max |\ln(x) - \widehat{\ln}(x)|$ is analytically bounded by $3.6 \times 10^{-4}$ ($<0.22\%$ relative) across $0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$ in real arithmetic prior to quantization. For deep wings ($|S-K| > 0.15K$), the engine engages an 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm fallback (SRL15-padded to 33 cycles), preserving fixed datapath latency.
    \item \textbf{Horner Normal CDF:} $N(x)$ follows Abramowitz \& Stegun (7.1.26)~\cite{abramowitz1964handbook} with theoretical error $|\epsilon| \le 7.5 \times 10^{-8}$ for $x \ge 0$: $N(x) = 1 - Z(x)\sum_{i=1}^5 b_i t^i$, where scale factor $t = 1/(1+p|x|)$ is evaluated by a dedicated 33-cycle non-restoring divider ($t_1, t_2$ for $d_1, d_2$), followed by 5th-order Horner single-DSP MAC stages. The pipeline applies symmetry $N(-x) = 1 - N(x)$ and clamps output to $[0, 1]$, using standard fixed-point coefficients ($p=0.2316419$, $b_1 \dots b_5$~\cite{abramowitz1964handbook}) and rounding in the repository artifact.
    \item \textbf{Hyperbolic CORDIC:} Exponentials $e^{-rT}$ and $e^{-d_1^2/2}$ are computed using a fully pipelined 16-stage hyperbolic CORDIC engine.
\end{itemize}

\section{Hardware Microarchitecture}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} shows the top-level architecture, comprising four identical cores coordinated by round-robin ingress and an AXI4-Stream egress arbiter.

\subsection{Stage 1: Ingress Registration \& Queuing (1 Cycle)}
The ingress interface receives a 256-bit AXI4-Stream packet containing $\{S, K, T, r, C_{\text{mkt}}, \text{TID}\}$. Ingress values are registered in 1 clock cycle and tagged with a 6-bit TID. Normalization division is omitted; the Pad\'{e} ratio $\frac{S-K}{S+K} \equiv \frac{\tilde{S}-1}{\tilde{S}+1}$ is evaluated inside Stage 3, saving 32 cycles.

\subsection{Stage 2: Analytical Guess Seeder \& Shared Sqrt Unit (64 Cycles)}
The seeder evaluates Eq.~(4): $\sigma_0 = \frac{2.5066\, C_{\text{mkt}}}{\sqrt{T} \cdot (S + K)/2}$. A digit-recurrence unit computes $\sqrt{T}$ in 29 cycles (plus alignment), storing $\sqrt{T}$ in distributed context RAM and forwarding it downstream, eliminating a redundant square-root core. An internal non-restoring divider evaluates the ratio in 33 cycles, producing a valid $\sigma_0$ in exactly 64 cycles.

\subsection{Stage 3: Core Pipelined Black-Scholes Datapath (126 Cycles)}
The core Stage-3 datapath is a 126-cycle fully unrolled feedforward pipeline measured from input register to output register (including all delay-matching SRLs and CORDIC padding, verified in cycle-accurate RTL simulation), accepting one contract/cycle ($II = 1$). Sub-operations are partitioned across registered boundaries. For deep wings ($|S-K| > 0.15K$), the 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm is latency-matched via SRL15 to 33 cycles, maintaining a fixed 126-cycle latency for each Stage-3 pass.

\subsection{Zero-BRAM Context Memory Architecture}
Conventional designs map FIFOs to Block RAMs (RAMB18/36). Our engine implements all context memories ($64 \times 32$-bit) exclusively using distributed LUTRAM (\texttt{ram\_style = "distributed"}) and SRL32 registers, consuming 0 Block RAMs/UltraRAMs in the accelerator core. Feedforward state flows through SRL32s ($II=1$), while distributed stores retain static parameters $\{S, K, C_{\text{mkt}}, r, T\}$ across transaction lifetimes (227 cycles cold, 386 loopback) to reinject market data for NR updates. Each core maintains an independent 64-slot store ($4 \times 64 = 256$ slots), governed by an \texttt{in\_flight\_count} counter and a 64-bit TID scoreboard (\texttt{tid\_busy\_mask}) asserting ingress backpressure (\texttt{s\_axis\_tready = 0}) when in-flight contracts reach $\ge 60$ slots ($\ge 4$ headroom slots) or the requested TID is active. Because loopback retains its pre-allocated slot with strict priority over new ingress, re-entry cannot be blocked by slot reclamation, preventing buffer deadlock; new-ingress fairness is preserved by the bounded loopback rate and 4-slot headroom policy. On Artix-7, this consumes 1,256 LUTRAM and 1,804 SRL32s within context memories (314 LUTRAM and 451 SRL32 per core), with 6,852 SRLs across the datapath for delay matching (8,656 total SRLs, 18.74\%); on Alveo U50, it occupies 0.28\% of LUTs, leaving all BRAM/URAM blocks available for external packet/DMA integration (PHY/MAC/PCIe are outside the boundary).

\subsection{Stage 4: Newton-Raphson Update Step \& Greek Dividers (33 Cycles)}
Following Stage 3, each core checks convergence $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| \le \epsilon$, where $\epsilon = 32'd167772 \approx \$0.0100$ in Q8.24, chosen as a representative \$0.01 price-residual hardware criterion. Note that this residual stopping criterion is an exchange convention rather than a uniform IV accuracy guarantee, as price error scales inversely with Vega ($\Delta\sigma \approx \Delta C / \nu$). The reported volatility-error statistics therefore represent empirical measurements of the complete fixed-point solver. If $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| > \epsilon$, update $\Delta\sigma = (C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}})/\nu$ is evaluated by a dedicated 33-cycle divider (\texttt{u\_nr\_divider}) concurrently with the 33-cycle Gamma divider (\texttt{u\_gamma\_divider}). To prevent oscillation and divergence as Vega $\nu \to 0$, the core incorporates hardware clamping: NR step bounded by $|\Delta\sigma| \le 0.25$ (\texttt{MAX\_STEP\_Q24}), volatility clamped to $[0.01, 5.00]$, seeder output clamped to $[0.05, 3.00]$, and \texttt{u\_nr\_divider} saturates upon zero denominator. Updated $\sigma_{n+1} = \sigma_n - \Delta\sigma$ loops back with priority via an active TID scoreboard. Because Greeks are registered to egress solely upon convergence, contracts requiring a second pass re-evaluate $\Delta$, $\nu$, and $\Gamma$ at the refined $\sigma_{n+1}$, ensuring all exported sensitivities strictly correspond to the final converged implied volatility.

\subsection{Stage 5: Multi-Core Arbitration \& 128-Bit Egress (2 Cycles)}
Upon convergence, results are packed into a 128-bit word: $\text{Egress}[127:0] = \{ \text{TID}[5:0],\, \Gamma[25:0],\, \nu[31:0],\, \Delta[31:0],\, \sigma[31:0] \}$. $\Gamma$ occupies 26 bits (signed Q2.24, $[-2.0, +1.999]$) by truncating 6 redundant sign bits from Q8.24; while bounded analytically by $\Gamma \le 1/(S\sigma\sqrt{2\pi T})$, this representation is empirically confirmed without saturation across all 11,382 evaluated contracts (max observed: 0.2357), with hardware clamping guarding extreme inputs. A 4-way arbiter drains output FIFOs in 2 cycles. Each core enforces local TID uniqueness across its 64 context slots ($4 \times 64 = 256$ slots total) via \texttt{tid\_busy\_mask[63:0]}, backpressuring ingress when a local TID is active or $\ge 60$ slots are occupied; the arbiter appends the 2-bit core index at the egress boundary to form an 8-bit global identifier $\{\text{Core}[1:0], \text{TID}[5:0]\}$, uniquely resolving all 256 in-flight transactions without a reorder buffer.

\subsection{Pipeline Latency and Sustained Throughput Reconciliation}
We distinguish between four operational terms: a \textbf{contract} denotes one input option quote; a \textbf{pass} denotes one evaluation through the Stage-3/Stage-4 datapath; an \textbf{iteration} denotes an unseeded Newton-Raphson feedback cycle; and an \textbf{operation (Op)} denotes one fully converged contract producing final IV and all three Greeks ($\Delta, \nu, \Gamma$). Stage-3 $II=1$ describes feedforward datapath acceptance, while sustained operation throughput reflects completed contracts adjusted by the average number of passes per contract. Cold transaction latency from ingress to egress is $L_{\text{cold}} = 1\,(\text{Stg 1}) + 64\,(\text{Stg 2}) + 1\,(\text{Q}) + 126\,(\text{Stg 3}) + 33\,(\text{Stg 4}) + 2\,(\text{Stg 5}) = \mathbf{227\text{ cycles}}$ (2.27~$\mu$s on Artix-7 / 0.91~$\mu$s on U50; isolated Stage-3 core datapath is 126~cycles, 1.26~$\mu$s / 0.50~$\mu$s). Loopback bypasses Stages~1--2, requiring Stages~3--4 ($126 + 33 = \mathbf{159\text{ cycles}}$, 1.59~$\mu$s / 0.64~$\mu$s), yielding a 386-cycle two-pass total (3.86~$\mu$s / 1.54~$\mu$s). Loopback does not stall the Stage-3 $II=1$ datapath; however, each loopback consumes one ingress scheduling opportunity. During the 159-cycle loopback pass, the core processes 158 subsequent new contracts, with only one ingress slot displaced by contract $k$'s re-entry.

\textbf{Liquid Trading ($S/K \in [0.85, 1.15]$):} Across 4,394 liquid contracts, 4,289 (97.6\%) converge in 1 pass and 105 (2.4\%) require 2 passes (averaging 1.024 slots/contract; zero convergence failures). Across 4 cores at 100~MHz, net sustained throughput is $T_{\text{net, liquid}} = \frac{400.00\text{ MOps/s}}{1.024} = \mathbf{390.62\text{ MOps/s}}$.

\textbf{Extended-Domain Throughput:} For contracts in the deep-wing regime ($|S/K - 1| > 0.15$), the CORDIC logarithm fallback engages and Vega diminishes, requiring an empirical average of 2.100 passes per contract (100\% convergence under hardware clamping). For the extended-domain evaluation suite (56.06\% deep-wing [5,606 contracts], 43.94\% liquid [4,394 contracts]), the blended scheduling cost is $\bar{P} = 0.4394(1.024) + 0.5606(2.100) = 1.627$ slots/contract, yielding \textbf{245.8~MOps/s net throughput} on Artix-7 and \textbf{614.4~MOps/s} on Alveo U50.

\subsection{Critical Timing Path Analysis}
On Artix-7 200T, timing closed at 100.00~MHz ($T_{\text{clk}} = 10.0\text{ ns}$) across 189,999 endpoints on the -3 speed grade at $T_j = 31.2\,^\circ\text{C}$. Multi-corner STA verifies both Slow ($WNS = +0.005\text{ ns}$, 0 fails) and Fast corners ($WHS = +0.058\text{ ns}$, 0 fails). Across multiple P\&R runs at 79.45\% LUT density, setup slack converges in $WNS \in [+0.002, +0.008]\text{ ns}$ (critical path 9.777~ns: 3.775~ns logic, 6.002~ns routing over 22 levels at Core~1 divider \texttt{SLICE\_X48Y87}).

For the evaluated four-core implementation, 100~MHz was the highest successfully closed frequency: higher constraints fail at 150~MHz ($WNS = -4.849\text{ ns}$) from routing congestion, whereas an isolated single core achieves $WNS = +0.658\text{ ns}$ ($F_{\max} \approx 106\text{ MHz}$).

On Alveo U50, 16nm FinFET scaling reduces critical path delay to 3.910~ns, enabling closure at 250.00~MHz ($WNS = +0.073\text{ ns}$, zero failing, $F_{\max} = 254.65\text{ MHz}$). The Artix-7 result represents a nominal closure point with 5~ps setup margin; environmental variations were not experimentally evaluated.

\subsection{Power and Thermal Budget}
Vivado post-route vectorless power analysis yields an estimated dissipation of 4.258~W (4.126~W dynamic, 0.132~W static) at $T_j = 31.2^\circ$C on Artix-7 200T (17\% of 25~W SmartNIC budget). On Alveo U50, the 4-core array consumes an estimated 9.560~W (7.214~W dynamic, 2.346~W static, $T_j = 30.0^\circ\text{C}$), delivering 1.00~GOps/s at 9.56~nJ/op (0.28\% LUTRAM reflects U50's larger capacity; physical shunt measurement is planned for journal work).

\subsection{Illustrative First-Order Error-Budget Analysis}
The observed MAE of $1.57 \times 10^{-4}$ ($0.0157\%$ vol) is examined via an illustrative first-order perturbation budget ($\Delta\sigma \approx \Delta C / \nu$): (1)~\textit{Pad\'{e} Logarithm ($\Delta_{\ln}$)}: $|u| \le 0.15$ bounds error to $\le 3.6 \times 10^{-4}$, inducing $\Delta\sigma_{\text{Pad\'{e}}} \approx 4.5 \times 10^{-5}$ ($0.0045\%$ vol); (2)~\textit{Hyperbolic CORDIC ($e^{-rT}, e^{-d_1^2/2}$)}: 16-stage precision ($2^{-16}$) induces $\Delta C \approx 7.6 \times 10^{-6} K$, giving $\Delta\sigma_{\text{CORDIC}} \approx 2.8 \times 10^{-5}$ for $\nu \approx 0.35 K$; (3)~\textit{Horner CDF \& Quantization}: 5th-order Horner error ($|\epsilon| \le 7.5 \times 10^{-8}$) yields $\Delta\sigma_{\text{Horner}} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-6}$, with Q8.24 roundoff uncertainty $\approx 2.5 \times 10^{-6}$; and (4)~\textit{Termination}: with mean $S=\$50, \bar{T}=0.8$ ($\bar{\nu} \approx \$17.35$), mean termination error $\overline{|\Delta C|} \approx \$0.0025$ yields $\Delta\sigma_{\text{stop}} \approx \frac{\$0.0025}{\$17.35} \approx 1.45 \times 10^{-4}$. Root-sum-square yields $\approx 1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ ($0.015\%$ vol), providing an illustrative check with the measured DPI-C MAE (terms represent dimensionless volatility errors under liquid conditions; not a formal worst-case bound).

\subsection{Algorithmic Ablation: Seeding vs. Unseeded Newton-Raphson}

Both variants execute on the identical RTL datapath with matching fixed-point rounding, clamping limits, $\$0.01$ threshold, and 8-pass ceiling; only the initial guess mechanism differs.

\subsection{Deployment Considerations and Scaling Projection}
Deployment spans two tiers: (1)~\textit{Tier-1 Inline Filtering} ($<$25~W PCIe mezzanine), where Artix-7 consumes 4.258~W (17\% of 25~W budget) leaving BRAM available for MAC/DMA; and (2)~\textit{Tier-2 Centralized Co-Processing} (75--225~W). The accelerator core consumes 0 Block RAM/UltraRAM, leaving on-chip memory unconstrained for potential co-location of 100GbE CMAC buffers or order-book tracking; physical network/host interfaces remain outside the core measurement boundary.

\section{Conclusion}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
We presented a four-core FPGA accelerator for streaming Black-Scholes IV solving and concurrent Greeks ($\Delta, \nu, \Gamma$). Synergizing Brenner-Subrahmanyam seeding, Q8.24 scale-invariance normalization, and a zero-BRAM microarchitecture, the engine achieves nominal post-route timing closure at 100~MHz on Artix-7 200T (400~MOps/s, 4.258~W) and 250~MHz on Alveo U50 (1.00~GOps/s, 9.560~W) with 0 BRAM/URAM in the accelerator core. DPI-C co-simulation confirms an IV MAE of $1.57 \times 10^{-4}$ near-the-money ($8.80 \times 10^{-4}$ liquid) with 99.9\% of evaluated contracts within 1.0\% vol error. The design provides cycle-deterministic per-pass execution (0.91~$\mu$s cold path on U50; complete transaction latency is pass-dependent), achieving 28.1$\times$ higher peak throughput and a 297$\times$ throughput-to-power ratio relative to the 16-thread AVX2 companion CPU baseline under post-route modeling assumptions.
