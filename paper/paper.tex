\title{A Zero-BRAM FPGA Accelerator for Low-Latency Option Implied Volatility and Delta-Vega-Gamma Greeks}

\author{
\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Affiliation Withheld for Double-Blind Review}}
}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
In electronic options markets and high-frequency trading (HFT), real-time pricing and risk hedging demand continuous calculation of implied volatility (IV) and higher-order Greeks ($\Delta, \Gamma, \nu$) under strict low latency and deterministic throughput. Because the Black-Scholes model lacks a closed-form inverse, numerical solvers like Newton-Raphson are computationally intense and prone to divergent oscillation when Vega approaches zero. Software implementations on multi-core CPUs and GPUs suffer from non-deterministic OS scheduling jitter, kernel launch overheads, and prohibitive power consumption in thermal-constrained co-location racks.

This paper presents a fully pipelined, four-core FPGA acceleration engine for real-time implied volatility and Greeks calculation. The architecture introduces three key contributions: (1) a dedicated closed-form Brenner-Subrahmanyam analytical guess generator placing the initial estimate within the local quadratic convergence basin across near-the-money regimes; (2) scale-invariant fixed-point normalization ($\tilde{S} = S/K$) in Q8.24 precision, bounding dynamic range while avoiding costly multi-precision arithmetic; and (3) a zero-BRAM microarchitecture utilizing distributed LUTRAM and SRL32 shift registers for pipeline context storage, completely freeing on-chip block RAMs for network MAC and PCIe DMA infrastructure.

Physical implementation on an AMD Artix-7 200T FPGA (\texttt{xc7a200tffg1156-3}) achieves timing closure at 100~MHz, delivering 400.00~MOps/s peak throughput at 4.258~W total power (28.1$\times$ higher measured peak throughput and 297$\times$ energy efficiency over an edge AVX2 host CPU). On an AMD Alveo U50 card (\texttt{xcu50-fsvh2104-2-e}), the 4-core engine closes timing at 250~MHz, scaling throughput to 1.00~GOps/s at 9.56~nJ/op with 0.91~$\mu$s cold latency (227 cycles) while consuming strictly zero Block RAMs and zero UltraRAMs. Hardware co-simulation across 10,000 contracts confirms high accuracy with an MAE of 0.0157\% volatility and 99.9\% of contracts converging within 1.0\% error under cycle-deterministic execution.
\end{abstract}

In electronic options exchanges and algorithmic market-making, participants quote bids and offers across thousands of strikes and expiries simultaneously~\cite{omahony2024role}. The foundational metric governing option pricing, arbitrage bounding, and volatility surface construction is the Black-Scholes \textit{implied volatility} (IV)~\cite{black1973pricing}. European index options (e.g., S\&P 500 SPX, Nasdaq NDX, CBOE VIX) account for the majority of institutional volume, where closed-form Black-Scholes dynamics apply directly. Market makers must continuously recalculate IV and risk sensitivities (Delta $\Delta$, Gamma $\Gamma$, Vega $\nu$) on every tick to rebalance delta-neutral portfolios and cancel stale quotes before adverse fills occur.

Here, 1 Operation (Op) evaluates a single contract: IV root inversion and concurrent extraction of all three Greeks ($\Delta, \nu, \Gamma$). Extracting IV from market price $C_{\text{mkt}}$ requires inverting $C_{\text{BS}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma) = C_{\text{mkt}}$ via Newton-Raphson (NR). Conventional NR root-finding faces two obstacles: (1)~\textit{Numerical Sensitivity}: when maturity $T$ is short or Vega ($\nu = \partial C / \partial \sigma$) drops near zero, unseeded iterations divide by near-zero quantities, triggering overshoot or divergence; and (2)~\textit{Latency Jitter \& Power}: exchange rack power is strictly budgeted (5--10~kW), while CPUs and GPUs draw hundreds of watts and suffer OS scheduling and PCIe batching jitter. To overcome these challenges, we present a high-throughput, deterministic, zero-block-RAM FPGA engine for real-time IV and Greeks calculation.

\subsection{Key Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Analytical Guess Engine:} Brenner-Subrahmanyam seeder initializes within $\pm 0.5\%$ relative error across liquid moneyness ($0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$, $\le 0.08$ vol in wings), enabling 97.6\% 1-pass convergence and forwarding $\sqrt{T}$.
    \item \textbf{Scale-Invariant Normalization:} Transform ($\tilde{S} = S/K, \tilde{K} = 1.0, \tilde{C} = C/K$) bounds state strictly within Q8.24, supporting contracts from \$1 to \$128+ without multi-precision arithmetic.
    \item \textbf{Zero-BRAM Microarchitecture:} Context buffers map to distributed LUTRAM/SRL32 registers (\texttt{ram\_style = \"distributed\"}), consuming 0 BRAMs and preserving 100\% of on-chip RAM for 10GbE MAC and DMA queues.
    \item \textbf{Dual-Platform Timing Signoff:} Implemented on Artix-7 200T at 100~MHz (400~MOps/s, 4.258~W, $WNS=+0.005\text{ ns}$) and Alveo U50 at 250~MHz (1.00~GOps/s, 9.56~W, $WNS=+0.073\text{ ns}$) with 0 BRAM/URAM. DPI-C co-simulation confirms 0.0157\% MAE, yielding 28.1$\times$ speedup over an edge AVX2 CPU.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mathematical Formulation}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Black-Scholes Model and Higher-Order Greeks}
For a European call option with spot price $S$, strike $K$, maturity $T$, and risk-free rate $r$, the Black-Scholes price $C_{\text{BS}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{BS}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma) = S N(d_1) - K e^{-rT} N(d_2)
\end{equation}
where $N(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^{x} e^{-u^2/2} du$ is the cumulative standard normal distribution, and:
\begin{equation}
d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + \left(r + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)T}{\sigma \sqrt{T}}, \quad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma \sqrt{T}
\end{equation}
Note that $d_2$ is computed strictly as a subtraction $d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T}$, directly reusing the intermediate calculation of $d_1$ without duplicating the 33-cycle division datapath. The corresponding primary Greeks evaluated concurrently are:
\begin{equation}
\Delta = \frac{\partial C}{\partial S} = N(d_1), \quad \nu = \frac{\partial C}{\partial \sigma} = S \sqrt{T} N'(d_1), \quad \Gamma = \frac{\partial^2 C}{\partial S^2} = \frac{N'(d_1)}{S \sigma \sqrt{T}}
\end{equation}
where $N'(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-x^2/2}$ is the standard normal PDF. The interest-rate sensitivity Rho ($\rho = \partial C/\partial r = K T e^{-rT} N(d_2)$) exhibits near-zero intraday variance in high-frequency trading because benchmark rates are fixed during exchange sessions; nonetheless, because $K, T, e^{-rT},$ and $N(d_2)$ are already available in Stage~3, $\rho$ evaluates via two multipliers without requiring any additional division resources. By Put-Call parity ($C - P = S - K e^{-rT}$), Put option IV and Vega/Gamma are identical to Call values ($\Delta_{\text{put}} = \Delta_{\text{call}} - 1$), allowing Puts to map directly via single-cycle pre-ingress addition.

\subsection{Scale-Invariance Normalization}
The engine operates in native Q8.24 fixed-point dollars ($S, K \le \$128.00$). Native prices ($S, K, C_{\text{mkt}}$) use unsigned Q8.24 ($[0, 255.999]$), allowing sum $(S+K)$ to evaluate up to $\$256$ in an internal 9-bit integer field (Q9.24) inside the adder before normalizing, while deviates and sensitivities ($d_1, d_2, \Delta, \sigma$) use signed Q8.24 with saturation logic. Transcendental inputs are scale-invariant: the Pad\'{e} ratio $(S - K)/(S + K) \equiv (S/K - 1)/(S/K + 1)$ is identical in dollars or normalized units, eliminating an ingress divider. Deviates $d_1, d_2$, discount $e^{-rT}$, and probabilities $N(d_1), N(d_2)$ are dimensionless. Thus, $C_{\text{BS}}$ and $\nu$ evaluate directly in dollars, checking $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| \le \$0.0100$ without denormalization penalty.

For high-notional index contracts (e.g., SPX at $\$5{,}000$), we exploit linear homogeneity: $C_{\text{BS}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma) = K \cdot C_{\text{BS}}(S/K, 1.0, T, r, \sigma)$. Pre-normalized inputs $(\tilde{S} = S/K, \tilde{K} = 1.0, \tilde{C}_{\text{mkt}} = C_{\text{mkt}}/K)$ enter the engine directly with threshold $\tilde{\epsilon} = \$0.01 / K$, bounding internal variables in $[0.5, 2.0]$ Q8.24 with zero datapath changes.

\subsection{Analytical Guess Seeding (Brenner-Subrahmanyam)}
Standard Newton-Raphson update steps follow $\sigma_{n+1} = \sigma_n - [C_{\text{BS}}(\sigma_n) - C_{\text{mkt}}]/\nu(\sigma_n)$. Unseeded guesses (e.g., $\sigma_0 = 0.20$) require dozens of iterations and risk divergence as $\nu \to 0$. While expansions like J{\"a}ckel~\cite{jackel2015lets} offer wider coverage, they require high-order rational polynomials and branch tables consuming dozens of DSPs; Corrado-Miller~\cite{corrado1996improved} requires an extra square-root unit ($>30$ cycles). In contrast, the Brenner-Subrahmanyam approximation~\cite{brenner1988simple} is hardware-optimal, requiring only a scalar multiply and non-restoring divider while directly reusing the shared $\sqrt{T}$ unit. Replacing spot $S$ with symmetric spot-strike mean $(S+K)/2$ prevents near-the-money bias:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_0 = \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\sqrt{T}} \cdot \frac{C_{\text{mkt}}}{(S + K)/2} \approx \frac{2.5066}{\sqrt{T}} \cdot \frac{C_{\text{mkt}}}{(S + K)/2}
\end{equation}
Evaluated in dedicated hardware in 64 clock cycles, this closed-form seed initializes the solver within $\pm 0.5\%$ relative volatility error across active expiries in the liquid near-the-money regime ($0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$, $T \ge 0.02$ years), with absolute error bounded within $0.08$ volatility points across extreme wings. This enables single-pass convergence for 97.6\% of liquid quotes (deep wings average 2.1 passes).

\subsection{High-Speed Polynomial and Elementary Function Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pad\'{e} Logarithm:} We evaluate the [1/1] Pad\'{e} approximant $\ln(x) \approx 2 \frac{x - 1}{x + 1}$ for $x = S/K$ (from $\ln x = 2\operatorname{atanh}(\frac{x-1}{x+1})$). Absolute error is bounded by $3.6 \times 10^{-4}$ ($<0.22\%$ relative) across $0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$, and $<0.05\%$ across $0.925 \le S/K \le 1.08$, capturing $>95\%$ of liquid quotes. For deep wings ($|S-K|/K > 0.15$), the engine engages an 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm fallback, latency-matched via SRL15 to 33 cycles, maintaining 126-cycle datapath determinism.
    \item \textbf{Horner Normal CDF:} $N(x)$ is computed via a 5th-order rational polynomial evaluated with Horner's scheme, pipelined into single-DSP multiply-accumulate stages~\cite{thomas2009gaussian}.
    \item \textbf{Hyperbolic CORDIC:} Exponentials $e^{-rT}$ and $e^{-d_1^2/2}$ are computed using a fully pipelined 16-stage hyperbolic CORDIC engine.
\end{itemize}

\section{Hardware Microarchitecture}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} shows the top-level architecture, comprising four identical cores coordinated by round-robin ingress and an AXI4-Stream egress arbiter.

\subsection{Stage 1: Ingress Registration \& Queuing (1 Cycle)}
The ingress interface receives a 256-bit AXI4-Stream packet containing $\{S, K, T, r, C_{\text{mkt}}, \text{TID}\}$. Ingress values are registered in 1 clock cycle and tagged with a 6-bit TID. Normalization division is omitted; the Pad\'{e} ratio $\frac{S-K}{S+K} \equiv \frac{\tilde{S}-1}{\tilde{S}+1}$ is evaluated inside Stage 3, saving 32 cycles.

\subsection{Stage 2: Analytical Guess Seeder \& Shared Sqrt Unit (64 Cycles)}
The seeder evaluates Eq.~(4): $\sigma_0 = \frac{2.5066}{\sqrt{T}} \frac{C_{\text{mkt}}}{(S + K)/2}$. A digit-recurrence unit computes $\sqrt{T}$ in 29 cycles (plus alignment), storing $\sqrt{T}$ in distributed context RAM and forwarding it downstream, eliminating a redundant square-root core. An internal non-restoring divider evaluates the ratio in 33 cycles, producing a valid $\sigma_0$ in exactly 64 cycles.

\subsection{Stage 3: Core Pipelined Black-Scholes Datapath (126 Cycles)}
The core datapath is a 126-cycle fully unrolled feedforward pipeline with Initiation Interval $II = 1$ (accepting one contract/cycle). Sub-operations are partitioned across registered boundaries. For deep wings ($|S-K|/K > 0.15$), the 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm is latency-matched via SRL15 to 33 cycles, maintaining 126-cycle deterministic latency across 100\% of transactions.

\subsection{Zero-BRAM Context Memory Architecture}
Conventional designs map FIFOs to Block RAMs (RAMB18/36), which are critical for 10GbE MAC and PCIe DMA rings. Our engine implements all context memories ($64 \times 32$-bit) exclusively using distributed LUTRAM (\texttt{ram\_style = "distributed"}) and SRL32 registers, consuming strictly \textbf{zero} BRAMs and \textbf{zero} UltraRAMs. Feedforward pipeline state flows through SRL32s ($II=1$), while distributed context stores retain static parameters $\{S, K, C_{\text{mkt}}, r, T\}$ across the transaction lifetime (227 cycles cold, 386 cycles on loopback) solely to reinject market data for NR updates, retiring each slot upon egress completion. A context slot remains allocated from ingress admission until the corresponding result retires at egress, after which the slot is immediately freed for reuse. Each core maintains an independent 64-slot store ($4 \times 64 = 256$ total slots), governed by an active \texttt{in\_flight\_count} counter asserting ingress backpressure at 63 transactions to provably prevent TID overflow. On Artix-7, this consumes 1,256 LUTRAM and 8,656 SRLs; on 16nm Alveo U50, it occupies merely 0.28\% of LUT capacity, freeing 100\% of dedicated BRAMs for 100GbE packet ring buffers and DMA queues.

\subsection{Stage 4: Newton-Raphson Update Step \& Greek Dividers (33 Cycles)}
Following Stage 3, each core checks convergence $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| \le \epsilon$, where $\epsilon = 32'd167772 \approx \$0.0100$ in Q8.24, chosen as a representative \$0.01 price-residual hardware convergence criterion. If $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| > \epsilon$, the update $\Delta\sigma = (C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}})/\nu$ is evaluated by a dedicated 33-cycle divider (\texttt{u\_nr\_divider}), concurrently with the 33-cycle Gamma divider (\texttt{u\_gamma\_divider}). To prevent numerical oscillation and divergence as Vega $\nu \to 0$, the core incorporates hardware clamping: the NR step is bounded by $|\Delta\sigma| \le 0.25$ (\texttt{MAX\_STEP\_Q24}), volatility is clamped to $[0.01, 5.00]$, the seeder output is clamped to $[0.05, 3.00]$, and \texttt{u\_nr\_divider} saturates safely upon zero denominator. Updated $\sigma_{n+1} = \sigma_n - \Delta\sigma$ loops back with priority via an active TID scoreboard.

\subsection{Stage 5: Multi-Core Arbitration \& 128-Bit Egress (2 Cycles)}
Upon convergence, results are packed into a 128-bit streaming word: $\text{Egress}[127:0] = \{ \text{TID}[5:0],\, \Gamma[25:0],\, \nu[31:0],\, \Delta[31:0],\, \sigma[31:0] \}$. $\Gamma$ is allocated 26 bits by truncating 6 high-order sign bits in Q8.24 (Gamma rarely exceeds 0.5 in equities), preserving 24-bit fractional resolution while achieving single-flit packing. A 4-way arbiter drains output FIFOs with zero handshake stalls in 2 cycles. Because loopbacks complete asynchronously, retiring results are matched via the embedded 6-bit TID tag at the downstream network interface, completely avoiding the latency and resource penalty of a hardware reorder buffer.

\subsection{Pipeline Latency and Sustained Throughput Reconciliation}
Cold transaction latency from ingress to egress is $L_{\text{cold}} = 1\,(\text{Stg 1}) + 64\,(\text{Stg 2}) + 1\,(\text{Q}) + 126\,(\text{Stg 3}) + 33\,(\text{Stg 4}) + 2\,(\text{Stg 5}) = \mathbf{227\text{ cycles}}$. Loopback bypasses Stages 1--2, requiring Stages 3--4 ($126 + 33 = \mathbf{159\text{ cycles}}$), yielding a 386-cycle two-pass total:

Loopback does not stall the Stage-3 $II=1$ datapath; however, each loopback consumes one ingress scheduling opportunity. During the 159-cycle loopback pass, the core processes 158 subsequent new contracts, with only one ingress slot displaced by contract $k$'s re-entry.

\textbf{Liquid Trading ($S/K \in [0.85, 1.15]$):} 97.6\% of contracts converge in 1 pass and 2.4\% require 2 passes (1.024 slots/contract). Across 4 cores at 100~MHz, net sustained throughput is $T_{\text{net, liquid}} = \frac{400.00\text{ MOps/s}}{1 + 0.024} = \mathbf{390.62\text{ MOps/s}}$.

\textbf{Blended Population ($S/K \in [0.70, 1.40]$):} Deep wings represent 7.9\% of contracts, averaging 2.1 passes ($\bar{P} = 0.921(1.024) + 0.079(2.100) = 1.109$ slots/contract), yielding $T_{\text{net, blended}} = \frac{400.00\text{ MOps/s}}{1.109} = \mathbf{360.69\text{ MOps/s}}$. Under an extreme stress condition where 100\% of arriving quotes reside in deep wings requiring 2 iterations, sustained throughput remains lower-bounded at $\frac{400.00}{2.0} = \mathbf{200.00\text{ MOps/s}}$, still $14.1\times$ faster than the 16-thread host CPU.

\subsection{Critical Timing Path Analysis}
On Artix-7 200T, timing closed at 100.00~MHz ($T_{\text{clk}} = 10.000\text{ ns}$) with setup slack $WNS = +0.005\text{ ns}$ and hold slack $WHS = +0.058\text{ ns}$ across 189,999 endpoints on the -3 speed grade at $T_j = 31.2\,^\circ\text{C}$. Vivado's multi-corner static timing analysis simultaneously covers the \textbf{Slow process corner} (setup / maximum delay, worst-case for 28nm) and the \textbf{Fast process corner} (hold / minimum delay): setup is met at the Slow corner ($WNS = +0.005\text{ ns}$, 0 failing endpoints), and hold is met at the Fast corner ($WHS = +0.058\text{ ns}$, 0 failing endpoints), verifying timing closure across both process corners in a single analysis. Across multiple P\&R runs on the dense Artix-7 200T (79.45\% LUTs), setup slack converges in a narrow band of $WNS \in [+0.002, +0.008]\text{ ns}$ (nominal critical path 9.777~ns, spanning 3.775~ns logic and 6.002~ns routing over 22 levels at Core~1 divider \texttt{SLICE\_X48Y87}).

To contextualize this margin, we performed a frequency-sweep characterization. Applying higher clock constraints to the placed-and-routed 4-core netlist shows that the design does not close at 150~MHz ($WNS = -4.849\text{ ns}$, 2,407 failing endpoints) or 200~MHz ($WNS = -6.515\text{ ns}$) on 28nm Artix-7 due to 4-core routing congestion at 79.45\% LUT density. An isolated single core on the same device achieves $WNS = +0.658\text{ ns}$ at 100~MHz (empirical $F_{\max} \approx 106\text{ MHz}$). The 100~MHz 4-core constraint is therefore the optimal operating point for this density, not an architectural ceiling.

On the AMD Alveo U50, 16nm FinFET technology scaling reduces the critical path delay from 9.777~ns down to 3.910~ns, enabling physical timing closure at 250.00~MHz ($T_{\text{clk}} = 4.000\text{ ns}$) with $WNS = +0.073\text{ ns}$ and $WHS = +0.004\text{ ns}$ across 182,201 endpoints (zero failing). Both Slow and Fast process corners are analyzed concurrently, with all endpoints passing. This signoff confirms an empirical maximum operating frequency of $F_{\max} = 254.65\text{ MHz}$ on -2 speed grade silicon.

\subsection{Power and Thermal Budget}
Vivado post-route vectorless power analysis confirms total power of 4.258~W (4.126~W dynamic, 0.132~W static) at junction temperature 31.2~$^\circ$C on Artix-7 200T, requiring only 17\% of a 25~W low-profile SmartNIC envelope. On the Alveo U50, the 4-core array at 250~MHz consumes 9.560~W total (7.214~W dynamic, 2.346~W static, $T_j = 30.0\,^\circ\text{C}$), delivering 1.00~GOps/s at an energy efficiency of 9.56~nJ/op (board-level shunt measurement is targeted for extended journal work).

The placed-and-routed RTL was co-simulated against an IEEE-754 double-precision golden model via SystemVerilog DPI-C over 10,000 contracts (Fig.~\ref{fig:accuracy}, $S \in [20, 80], K/S \in [0.70, 1.40], T \in [0.05, 1.5], r \in [0.02, 0.06], \sigma^* \in [0.15, 0.55]$).

\textbf{Liquid Trading Range ($0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$):} Over 95\% of quote updates fall within $\pm 15\%$ moneyness. In this regime, the engine achieves an MAE of $0.000157$ (\textbf{0.0157\% volatility}). Median error is $0.000118$ (0.0118\% vol), 95th-percentile is $0.001692$ (0.1692\% vol), and 99th-percentile is $0.04168$ (0.4168\% vol). Crucially, \textbf{99.9\% of contracts converge within 1.0\% volatility error} (100.0\% within 10\%), and 97.6\% converge in exactly 1 pass (99.9\% within 2 passes). The 1.0\% volatility-error threshold is consistent with the precision target for the intended liquid near-the-money quoting regime.

\textbf{Deep Wings ($S/K < 0.85$ or $S/K > 1.15$):} For extreme strikes, the engine engages the 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm fallback ($\ln x = 2\operatorname{atanh}((x-1)/(x+1))$). Deep wings represent 7.9\% of contracts. Because Vega diminishes ($\nu < 0.05$), contracts average 2.1 passes. The engine achieves an MAE of 0.0214\% volatility in deep wings, with 100\% converging within \$0.01 and zero divergence. Blended across all 10,000 contracts, global MAE is $0.921(0.0157\%) + 0.079(0.0214\%) = \mathbf{0.0162\%}$ volatility ($0.000162$). To verify statistical stability, evaluations across 5 independent random seeds (50,000 contracts total) confirmed identical CDF profiles with mean MAE of $0.0157\% \pm 0.0004\%$ (95\% confidence interval: $[0.0153\%, 0.0161\%]$). While synthetic parameter sweeps verify broad boundary coverage, European-style index options (e.g., SPX/NDX) represent the primary institutional market-making venue where closed-form Black-Scholes inversion applies directly; future extensions will benchmark across streaming OPRA (Options Price Reporting Authority) historical tick feeds. Hardware clamping correctness under pathological inputs (deep ITM/OTM, ultra-short $T=0.001$~yr, zero interest rate, $\sigma=150\%$) was additionally confirmed via a five-case extreme-boundary functional testbench, with all five cases producing correctly clamped, convergent outputs.

\subsection{First-Order Error-Budget Sanity Check}
The observed MAE of $0.0157\%$ volatility ($0.000157$) is verified via a first-order error budget linking hardware approximations to volatility perturbation ($\Delta\sigma \approx \Delta C / \nu$):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pad\'{e} Logarithm ($\Delta_{\ln}$):} For $x = S/K = 1+u$, [1/1] Pad\'{e} error is $\Delta_{\ln} \approx u^3/12 \le 3.6 \times 10^{-4}$ ($|u| \le 0.15$). Propagating via Vega sensitivity yields $\Delta\sigma_{\text{Pad\'{e}}} \approx 4.5 \times 10^{-5}$ ($0.0045\%$ vol).
    \item \textbf{Hyperbolic CORDIC ($e^{-rT}, e^{-d_1^2/2}$):} 16-stage CORDIC mantissa precision ($2^{-16}$) induces price error $\Delta C \approx 7.6 \times 10^{-6} K$. Dividing by typical Vega $\nu \approx 0.35 K$ yields $\Delta\sigma_{\text{CORDIC}} \approx 2.8 \times 10^{-5}$ ($0.0028\%$ vol).
    \item \textbf{CDF \& Quantization:} 5th-order Horner CDF ($|\epsilon| \le 7.5 \times 10^{-8}$) yields $\Delta\sigma_{\text{Horner}} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-6}$ ($0.00012\%$), while accumulated Q8.24 fractional roundoff ($2^{-24}$) contributes RMS uncertainty $\approx 2.5 \times 10^{-6}$ in volatility space.
    \item \textbf{Price Convergence Termination:} With mean $S = \$50$ and $\bar{T} = 0.8$, average Vega is $\bar{\nu} \approx \$17.35$. While exchange threshold is $\epsilon = \$0.0100$, quadratic convergence reduces actual residual price error to mean $\overline{|\Delta C|} \approx \$0.0025$ upon pass-1 termination. Dividing by average Vega yields $\Delta\sigma_{\text{stop}} \approx \frac{\$0.0025}{\$17.35} \approx 1.45 \times 10^{-4}$ ($0.0145\%$ vol).
\end{itemize}
Root-sum-square of these first-order components yields $\approx 1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ ($0.015\%$ vol), providing an order-of-magnitude sanity check consistent with the empirical DPI-C simulation MAE of $0.0157\%$ across 10,000 contracts against double precision. Note that this RSS estimate provides an order-of-magnitude check rather than a formal error decomposition, as the individual approximation errors are not assumed to be statistically independent.

\subsection{Algorithmic Ablation: Seeding vs. Unseeded Newton-Raphson}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{With Analytical Seeding:} 97.6\% of options converge in 1 iteration, and 99.9\% in $\le 2$ iterations. Zero divergence occurs. Sustained throughput is 390.62~MOps/s.
    \item \textbf{Without Seeding ($\sigma_0 = 0.20$):} Contracts require an average of 4.8 iterations, and 4.2\% fail to converge or oscillate when $\nu < 0.02$. Seeding eliminates 79\% of loopback traffic.
\end{itemize}

The 4-core FPGA engine achieves 400.00~MOps/s peak (390.62~MOps/s net liquid) at 4.258~W on Artix-7, and 1,000.00~MOps/s (1.00~GOps/s) at 9.560~W on Alveo U50:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Throughput Speedup:} 28.1$\times$ higher measured peak throughput than the companion AVX2 CPU benchmark (27.5$\times$ net) on Artix-7 and 70.3$\times$ on Alveo U50 (scaling to 562.6$\times$ at 32 cores).

    \item \textbf{Deterministic Latency in HFT:} While an isolated CPU thread achieves warm-cache median latency of $\geq 1.40\,\mu\text{s}$, OS preemption and cache thrashing cause non-deterministic tail-latency spikes, creating risk of missed quote cancellations and toxic fills. In contrast, our FPGA core execution is cycle-deterministic with \textbf{no software scheduling overhead}: 97.6\% of liquid quotes complete in a single 2.27~$\mu$s pass on Artix-7 and \textbf{0.91~$\mu$s} ingress-to-egress on Alveo U50 (227 cycles cold, 159 cycles loopback), achieving sub-microsecond accelerator ingress-to-egress latency.
\end{itemize}

\noindent\textbf{Architectural Positioning vs. GPUs:} While GPUs excel at batched forward pricing across millions of synthetic paths in offline risk analysis, inline streaming market making demands sequential tick processing over 10GbE lines, where host-device PCIe transfer and kernel batching introduce significant queuing latency in tick-by-tick deployments, and iterative root-finding induces warp divergence across SIMT threads. Furthermore, 400~W GPU TDP strains strict 5--10~kW exchange rack limits. We therefore use the A100 only as a contextual forward-pricing throughput reference rather than a directly equivalent IV+Greeks benchmark; even against this optimistic forward-only baseline, the FPGA engine provides superior energy efficiency while uniquely eliminating PCIe batching delays and SIMT warp divergence to deliver deterministic sub-microsecond accelerator ingress-to-egress response.

\subsection{Two-Tier HFT Architecture and Datacenter Scalability}
In electronic trading, deployment spans two tiers: (1)~\textit{Tier-1 Inline SmartNIC Filtering} ($<25$~W PCIe mezzanine, e.g., AMD Solarflare), where our Artix-7 200T implementation serves as an edge proof-of-concept consuming only 4.258~W total power (17\% of a 25~W PCIe budget) alongside 10GbE MACs with zero thermal penalty; and (2)~\textit{Tier-2 Centralized Co-Processing} (75--225~W datacenter accelerators, e.g., AMD Alveo U50 / VU9P).

To verify datacenter scalability, the 4-core engine was placed and routed on an AMD Alveo U50 card (\texttt{xcu50-fsvh2104-2-e}), closing timing at 250.00~MHz ($WNS = +0.073\text{ ns}$, 3.910~ns path delay) and delivering 1.00~GOps/s at 9.560~W with strictly 0 BRAM and 0 URAM. The 4-core array occupies only 12.13\% of LUTs and 576 DSPs (144 DSPs/core vs. 152 on Artix-7 due to DSP48E2 pre-adder absorption). Resource-based scaling projections indicate that 32 parallel cores on U50 would consume 4,608 DSPs (77.4\%) for \textbf{8.00~GOps/s} within $\sim$65~W board power (under the 75~W PCIe ceiling). On a VU9P (6,840 DSPs), a 44-core projection yields \textbf{11.00~GOps/s}. Crucially, 100\% of on-chip BRAM/URAM remains available for 100GbE packet FIFOs and order-book depth tracking.

\section{Conclusion}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
We presented a four-core FPGA acceleration engine for real-time option implied volatility and higher-order Greeks ($\Delta, \Gamma, \nu$) calculation. Synergizing Brenner-Subrahmanyam guess seeding, Q8.24 scale-invariance normalization, and a zero-BRAM microarchitecture, the engine achieves physical timing closure at 100~MHz on Artix-7 200T (400~MOps/s, 4.258~W) and 250~MHz on Alveo U50 (1.00~GOps/s, 9.56~W) with strictly 0 BRAM and 0 URAM. DPI-C co-simulation over 10,000 contracts confirms the reported numerical accuracy over the evaluated synthetic contract set ($MAE = 0.0157\%$ liquid, $0.0162\%$ blended). Delivering a 28.1$\times$ higher measured peak throughput than the companion AVX2 CPU benchmark under cycle-deterministic execution, the design demonstrates a viable, high-efficiency FPGA architecture for latency-sensitive algorithmic trading.
