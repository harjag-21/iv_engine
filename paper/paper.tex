\title{A Zero-BRAM FPGA Accelerator for Low-Latency Option Implied Volatility and Delta-Vega-Gamma Greeks}

\author{
\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Affiliation Withheld for Double-Blind Review}}
}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Real-time option implied-volatility inversion requires iterative numerical solving and is challenging to implement under strict latency and FPGA resource constraints. This paper presents a four-core, fully pipelined FPGA accelerator for Black-Scholes implied volatility with concurrent Delta, Vega, and Gamma computation. The architecture combines a Brenner-Subrahmanyam analytical seed with a shared $\sqrt{T}$ datapath, scale-invariant $S/K$ normalization using Q8.24 fixed point, and a zero-BRAM context architecture based on distributed LUTRAM and SRL32 storage. Post-route implementation achieves 400~MOps/s at 100~MHz on an Artix-7 200T and 1.00~GOps/s at 250~MHz on an Alveo U50, using zero BRAM and zero UltraRAM on the latter. DPI-C validation against an IEEE-754 double-precision reference over 10,000 synthetic contracts yields an absolute volatility MAE of $1.57 \times 10^{-4}$ (0.0157 percentage points), with 99.9\% of contracts within 1.0 percentage point error. The U50 implementation achieves 0.91-$\mu$s cold accelerator ingress-to-egress latency (227 cycles). These results demonstrate a resource-efficient FPGA architecture for low-latency streaming IV and Greeks computation.
\end{abstract}

\begin{IEEEkeywords}
FPGA Acceleration, High-Frequency Trading, Implied Volatility, Option Greeks, Black-Scholes Model, Zero-BRAM Architecture, Newton-Raphson Solver, Energy Efficiency.
\end{IEEEkeywords}

\section{Introduction}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{figure*}[t]

\label{fig:arch}

In electronic options exchanges and algorithmic market-making, participants quote bids and offers across thousands of strikes and expiries simultaneously~\cite{omahony2024role}. The foundational metric governing option pricing, arbitrage bounding, and volatility surface construction is the Black-Scholes \textit{implied volatility} (IV)~\cite{black1973pricing}. European index options (e.g., S\&P 500 SPX, Nasdaq NDX, CBOE VIX) represent widely traded benchmark contracts where European-style exercise dynamics apply directly~\cite{cboe2023specs}. Market makers must continuously recalculate IV and risk sensitivities (Delta $\Delta$, Gamma $\Gamma$, Vega $\nu$) on every tick to rebalance delta-neutral portfolios and cancel stale quotes before adverse fills occur.

Here, 1 Operation (Op) evaluates a single contract: IV root inversion and concurrent extraction of all three Greeks ($\Delta, \nu, \Gamma$). Extracting IV from market price $C_{\text{mkt}}$ requires inverting $C_{\text{BS}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma) = C_{\text{mkt}}$ via Newton-Raphson (NR). Conventional NR root-finding faces two obstacles: (1)~\textit{Numerical Sensitivity}: when maturity $T$ is short or Vega ($\nu = \partial C / \partial \sigma$) drops near zero, unseeded iterations divide by near-zero quantities, triggering overshoot or divergence; and (2)~\textit{Latency Jitter \& Power}: conventional CPUs and GPUs draw high device power and suffer from non-deterministic OS scheduling and PCIe transfer jitter in single-tick streaming scenarios. To overcome these challenges, we present a high-throughput, deterministic, zero-block-RAM FPGA engine for real-time IV and Greeks calculation.

\subsection{Key Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Analytical Guess Engine:} Brenner-Subrahmanyam seeder initializes within relative error $\le 0.5\%$ in the liquid near-the-money regime ($0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$, $\le 0.08$ absolute vol in wings), enabling 97.6\% 1-pass convergence while forwarding shared $\sqrt{T}$.
    \item \textbf{Scale-Invariant Normalization:} Exploiting Black-Scholes linear homogeneity ($\tilde{S} = S/K, \tilde{K} = 1.0, \tilde{C} = C/K$) bounds dynamic range strictly within Q8.24, supporting high-notional contracts without multi-precision arithmetic.
    \item \textbf{Zero-BRAM Microarchitecture:} Context buffers map to distributed LUTRAM/SRL32 registers (\texttt{ram\_style = \"distributed\"}), consuming 0 BRAMs and leaving dedicated on-chip block RAMs completely available for external packet and DMA buffers.
    \item \textbf{Dual-Platform Timing Signoff:} Implemented on Artix-7 200T at 100~MHz (400~MOps/s, 4.258~W post-route power estimate, $WNS=+0.005\text{ ns}$) and Alveo U50 at 250~MHz (1.00~GOps/s, 9.56~W post-route estimate, $WNS=+0.073\text{ ns}$) with 0 BRAM/URAM. DPI-C co-simulation confirms $1.57 \times 10^{-4}$ absolute volatility MAE, yielding a peak throughput of 400~MOps/s (28.1$\times$ the companion AVX2 CPU throughput).
\end{itemize}

\section{Mathematical Formulation}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Black-Scholes Model and Higher-Order Greeks}
For a European call option with spot price $S$, strike $K$, maturity $T$, and risk-free rate $r$, the Black-Scholes price $C_{\text{BS}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{BS}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma) = S N(d_1) - K e^{-rT} N(d_2)
\end{equation}
where $N(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^{x} e^{-u^2/2} du$ is the cumulative standard normal distribution, and:
\begin{equation}
d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + \left(r + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)T}{\sigma \sqrt{T}}, \quad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma \sqrt{T}
\end{equation}
Note that $d_2$ is computed strictly as a subtraction $d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T}$, directly reusing intermediate $d_1$ without duplicating the 33-cycle divider. The primary Greeks ($\Delta = \partial C/\partial S$, $\nu = \partial C/\partial \sigma$, $\Gamma = \partial^2 C/\partial S^2$) evaluate concurrently as:
\begin{equation}
\Delta = N(d_1), \quad \nu = S \sqrt{T} N'(d_1), \quad \Gamma = \frac{N'(d_1)}{S \sigma \sqrt{T}}
\end{equation}
where $N'(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-x^2/2}$ is the standard normal PDF.

\subsection{Scale-Invariance Normalization}
The engine operates in native Q8.24 fixed-point dollars ($S, K \le \$128.00$). Native prices ($S, K, C_{\text{mkt}}$) use unsigned Q8.24 ($[0, 255.999]$), allowing sum $(S+K)$ to evaluate up to $\$256$ in an internal 9-bit integer field (Q9.24) inside the adder before normalizing, while deviates and sensitivities ($d_1, d_2, \Delta, \sigma$) use signed Q8.24 with saturation logic. Transcendental inputs are scale-invariant: the Pad\'{e} ratio $(S - K)/(S + K) \equiv (S/K - 1)/(S/K + 1)$ is identical in dollars or normalized units, eliminating an ingress divider. Deviates $d_1, d_2$, discount $e^{-rT}$, and probabilities $N(d_1), N(d_2)$ are dimensionless. Thus, $C_{\text{BS}}$ and $\nu$ evaluate directly in dollars, checking $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| \le \$0.0100$ without denormalization penalty.

For high-notional index contracts (e.g., SPX at $\$5{,}000$), we exploit linear homogeneity: $C_{\text{BS}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma) = K \cdot C_{\text{BS}}(S/K, 1.0, T, r, \sigma)$. Pre-normalized inputs $(\tilde{S} = S/K, \tilde{K} = 1.0, \tilde{C}_{\text{mkt}} = C_{\text{mkt}}/K)$ enter the engine directly with threshold $\tilde{\epsilon} = \$0.01 / K$, bounding the principal normalized price variables to a compact Q8.24 range without datapath widening.

\subsection{Analytical Guess Seeding (Brenner-Subrahmanyam)}
Standard Newton-Raphson update steps follow $\sigma_{n+1} = \sigma_n - [C_{\text{BS}}(\sigma_n) - C_{\text{mkt}}]/\nu(\sigma_n)$. Unseeded guesses (e.g., $\sigma_0 = 0.20$) can require substantially more iterations and exhibit oscillation or divergence when Vega $\nu \to 0$. While expansions like J{\"a}ckel~\cite{jackel2015lets} offer wider coverage, they require high-order rational polynomials and branch tables consuming dozens of DSPs; Corrado-Miller~\cite{corrado1996improved} requires an extra square-root unit ($>30$ cycles). In contrast, the Brenner-Subrahmanyam approximation~\cite{brenner1988simple} is hardware-optimal, requiring only a scalar multiply and non-restoring divider while directly reusing the shared $\sqrt{T}$ unit. Replacing spot $S$ with symmetric spot-strike mean $(S+K)/2$ prevents near-the-money bias:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_0 = \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\sqrt{T}} \cdot \frac{C_{\text{mkt}}}{(S + K)/2} \approx \frac{2.5066}{\sqrt{T}} \cdot \frac{C_{\text{mkt}}}{(S + K)/2}
\end{equation}
Evaluated in dedicated hardware in 64 clock cycles, this closed-form seed initializes the solver with relative error $\le 0.5\%$ across active expiries in the liquid near-the-money regime ($0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$, $T \ge 0.02$ years), and absolute error bounded within $0.08$ volatility points across extreme wings. This enables single-pass convergence for 97.6\% of liquid quotes (deep wings average 2.1 passes).

\subsection{High-Speed Polynomial and Elementary Function Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pad\'{e} Logarithm:} We evaluate the [1/1] Pad\'{e} approximant $\ln(x) \approx 2 \frac{x - 1}{x + 1}$ for $x = S/K$ (from $\ln x = 2\operatorname{atanh}(\frac{x-1}{x+1})$). Absolute error is bounded by $3.6 \times 10^{-4}$ ($<0.22\%$ relative) across $0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$, and $<0.05\%$ across $0.925 \le S/K \le 1.08$, capturing $>95\%$ of liquid quotes. For deep wings ($|S-K|/K > 0.15$), the engine engages an 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm fallback, latency-matched via SRL15 to 33 cycles, maintaining 126-cycle datapath determinism.
    \item \textbf{Horner Normal CDF:} $N(x)$ is computed via a 5th-order rational polynomial evaluated with Horner's scheme, pipelined into single-DSP multiply-accumulate stages~\cite{abramowitz1964handbook}.
    \item \textbf{Hyperbolic CORDIC:} Exponentials $e^{-rT}$ and $e^{-d_1^2/2}$ are computed using a fully pipelined 16-stage hyperbolic CORDIC engine.
\end{itemize}

\section{Hardware Microarchitecture}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} shows the top-level architecture, comprising four identical cores coordinated by round-robin ingress and an AXI4-Stream egress arbiter.

\subsection{Stage 1: Ingress Registration \& Queuing (1 Cycle)}
The ingress interface receives a 256-bit AXI4-Stream packet containing $\{S, K, T, r, C_{\text{mkt}}, \text{TID}\}$. Ingress values are registered in 1 clock cycle and tagged with a 6-bit TID. Normalization division is omitted; the Pad\'{e} ratio $\frac{S-K}{S+K} \equiv \frac{\tilde{S}-1}{\tilde{S}+1}$ is evaluated inside Stage 3, saving 32 cycles.

\subsection{Stage 2: Analytical Guess Seeder \& Shared Sqrt Unit (64 Cycles)}
The seeder evaluates Eq.~(4): $\sigma_0 = \frac{2.5066}{\sqrt{T}} \frac{C_{\text{mkt}}}{(S + K)/2}$. A digit-recurrence unit computes $\sqrt{T}$ in 29 cycles (plus alignment), storing $\sqrt{T}$ in distributed context RAM and forwarding it downstream, eliminating a redundant square-root core. An internal non-restoring divider evaluates the ratio in 33 cycles, producing a valid $\sigma_0$ in exactly 64 cycles.

\subsection{Stage 3: Core Pipelined Black-Scholes Datapath (126 Cycles)}
The core datapath is a 126-cycle fully unrolled feedforward pipeline with Initiation Interval $II = 1$ (accepting one contract/cycle). Sub-operations are partitioned across registered boundaries. For deep wings ($|S-K|/K > 0.15$), the 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm is latency-matched via SRL15 to 33 cycles, maintaining 126-cycle deterministic latency across 100\% of transactions.

\subsection{Zero-BRAM Context Memory Architecture}
Conventional designs map FIFOs to Block RAMs (RAMB18/36), which are critical for 10GbE MAC and PCIe DMA rings. Our engine implements all context memories ($64 \times 32$-bit) exclusively using distributed LUTRAM (\texttt{ram\_style = "distributed"}) and SRL32 registers, consuming strictly \textbf{zero} BRAMs and \textbf{zero} UltraRAMs. Feedforward pipeline state flows through SRL32s ($II=1$), while distributed context stores retain static parameters $\{S, K, C_{\text{mkt}}, r, T\}$ across transaction lifetimes (227 cycles cold, 386 loopback) solely to reinject market data for NR updates. A context slot remains allocated from ingress until egress retirement. Each core maintains an independent 64-slot store ($4 \times 64 = 256$ slots), governed by an active \texttt{in\_flight\_count} counter asserting ingress backpressure at 63 transactions to provably prevent TID overflow. On Artix-7, this consumes 1,256 LUTRAM and 8,656 SRLs; on 16nm Alveo U50, it occupies merely 0.28\% of LUT capacity, leaving dedicated BRAM/URAM blocks completely available for external 100GbE packet buffers and host DMA rings.

\subsection{Stage 4: Newton-Raphson Update Step \& Greek Dividers (33 Cycles)}
Following Stage 3, each core checks convergence $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| \le \epsilon$, where $\epsilon = 32'd167772 \approx \$0.0100$ in Q8.24, chosen as a representative \$0.01 price-residual hardware convergence criterion. If $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| > \epsilon$, the update $\Delta\sigma = (C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}})/\nu$ is evaluated by a dedicated 33-cycle divider (\texttt{u\_nr\_divider}), concurrently with the 33-cycle Gamma divider (\texttt{u\_gamma\_divider}). To prevent numerical oscillation and divergence as Vega $\nu \to 0$, the core incorporates hardware clamping: the NR step is bounded by $|\Delta\sigma| \le 0.25$ (\texttt{MAX\_STEP\_Q24}), volatility is clamped to $[0.01, 5.00]$, the seeder output is clamped to $[0.05, 3.00]$, and \texttt{u\_nr\_divider} saturates safely upon zero denominator. Updated $\sigma_{n+1} = \sigma_n - \Delta\sigma$ loops back with priority via an active TID scoreboard.

\subsection{Stage 5: Multi-Core Arbitration \& 128-Bit Egress (2 Cycles)}
Upon convergence, results are packed into a 128-bit streaming word: $\text{Egress}[127:0] = \{ \text{TID}[5:0],\, \Gamma[25:0],\, \nu[31:0],\, \Delta[31:0],\, \sigma[31:0] \}$. $\Gamma$ is allocated 26 bits by truncating 6 redundant high-order sign bits from Q8.24 (maximum observed Gamma across all 10,000 synthetic and 1,382 SPX contracts was 0.2357, safely within the $[-2.0, +1.999]$ representation range with zero saturation), preserving full 24-bit fractional resolution while achieving single-flit packing. A 4-way arbiter drains output FIFOs with zero handshake stalls in 2 cycles. Because loopbacks complete asynchronously, retiring results are matched via the embedded 6-bit TID tag at the downstream network interface, completely avoiding the latency and resource penalty of a hardware reorder buffer.

\subsection{Pipeline Latency and Sustained Throughput Reconciliation}
Cold transaction latency from ingress to egress is $L_{\text{cold}} = 1\,(\text{Stg 1}) + 64\,(\text{Stg 2}) + 1\,(\text{Q}) + 126\,(\text{Stg 3}) + 33\,(\text{Stg 4}) + 2\,(\text{Stg 5}) = \mathbf{227\text{ cycles}}$. Loopback bypasses Stages 1--2, requiring Stages 3--4 ($126 + 33 = \mathbf{159\text{ cycles}}$), yielding a 386-cycle two-pass total:

Loopback does not stall the Stage-3 $II=1$ datapath; however, each loopback consumes one ingress scheduling opportunity. During the 159-cycle loopback pass, the core processes 158 subsequent new contracts, with only one ingress slot displaced by contract $k$'s re-entry.

\textbf{Liquid Trading ($S/K \in [0.85, 1.15]$):} 97.6\% of contracts converge in 1 pass and 2.4\% require 2 passes (1.024 slots/contract). Across 4 cores at 100~MHz, net sustained throughput is $T_{\text{net, liquid}} = \frac{400.00\text{ MOps/s}}{1 + 0.024} = \mathbf{390.62\text{ MOps/s}}$.

\textbf{Extended-Domain Projection:} For contracts in the deep-wing regime ($|S/K - 1| > 0.15$), the 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm fallback engages and Vega diminishes ($\nu < 0.05$), requiring an average of 2.1 passes. A behavioral projection assuming 100\% deep-wing traffic gives $\frac{400.00}{2.0} = \mathbf{200.00\text{ MOps/s}}$ lower-bound throughput, still $14.1\times$ faster than the 16-thread host CPU. For a realistic population combining liquid and deep-wing contracts (e.g., 92.1\% liquid, 7.9\% wings), average slots/contract $\bar{P} = 0.921(1.024) + 0.079(2.100) = 1.109$, yielding $\mathbf{360.69\text{ MOps/s}}$ blended net throughput.

\subsection{Critical Timing Path Analysis}
On Artix-7 200T, timing closed at 100.00~MHz ($T_{\text{clk}} = 10.000\text{ ns}$) across 189,999 endpoints on the -3 speed grade at $T_j = 31.2\,^\circ\text{C}$. Vivado multi-corner STA simultaneously verifies the \textbf{Slow process corner} (setup/max delay: $WNS = +0.005\text{ ns}$, 0 fails) and \textbf{Fast process corner} (hold/min delay: $WHS = +0.058\text{ ns}$, 0 fails), demonstrating timing closure for the reported setup/hold corner analyses. Across multiple P\&R runs at 79.45\% LUT density, setup slack converges in $WNS \in [+0.002, +0.008]\text{ ns}$ (nominal critical path 9.777~ns: 3.775~ns logic, 6.002~ns routing over 22 levels at Core~1 divider \texttt{SLICE\_X48Y87}).

A frequency sweep confirms 100~MHz is optimal for this density: higher clock constraints fail at 150~MHz ($WNS = -4.849\text{ ns}$, 2,407 failing endpoints) and 200~MHz ($WNS = -6.515\text{ ns}$) due to 4-core routing congestion, whereas an isolated single core achieves $WNS = +0.658\text{ ns}$ at 100~MHz (empirical $F_{\max} \approx 106\text{ MHz}$).

On the AMD Alveo U50, 16nm FinFET technology scaling reduces the critical path delay from 9.777~ns down to 3.910~ns, enabling physical timing closure at 250.00~MHz ($T_{\text{clk}} = 4.000\text{ ns}$) with $WNS = +0.073\text{ ns}$ and $WHS = +0.004\text{ ns}$ across 182,201 endpoints (zero failing). Both Slow and Fast process corners are analyzed concurrently, with all endpoints passing. This signoff confirms an empirical maximum operating frequency of $F_{\max} = 254.65\text{ MHz}$ on -2 speed grade silicon.

\subsection{Power and Thermal Budget}
Vivado post-route vectorless power analysis yields an estimated total power dissipation of 4.258~W (4.126~W dynamic, 0.132~W static) at junction temperature 31.2~$^\circ$C on Artix-7 200T, occupying only 17\% of a 25~W low-profile SmartNIC power budget. On the Alveo U50, the 4-core array at 250~MHz consumes an estimated 9.560~W total (7.214~W dynamic, 2.346~W static, $T_j = 30.0\,^\circ\text{C}$), delivering 1.00~GOps/s at an energy efficiency of 9.56~nJ/op (physical board-level shunt measurement is targeted for extended journal work).

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includefigfile[width=0.67\columnwidth]{fig2_error_distribution.pdf}{fig1_error_distribution.pdf}
\caption{(a) Probability density error distribution and (b) cumulative accuracy curve across 10,000 synthetic contracts evaluated via DPI-C co-simulation.}
\label{fig:accuracy}
\end{figure}

Synthetic contract parameters $\{S, K/S, T, r, \sigma^*\}$ are generated via uniform, independent pseudo-random sampling across the liquid near-the-money regime ($S \in [20, 80]$, $K/S \in [0.90, 1.10]$, $T \in [0.10, 1.5]$~yr, $r \in [0.02, 0.06]$, $\sigma^* \in [0.15, 0.55]$), spanning 10,000 contracts with random seed 42. The placed-and-routed RTL was co-simulated against an IEEE-754 double-precision golden model via SystemVerilog DPI-C (Fig.~\ref{fig:accuracy}).

The RTL achieves a \textbf{mean absolute error (MAE) of $1.57 \times 10^{-4}$ absolute volatility} ($0.0157$ percentage points, 1.57 vol-bps), with \textbf{99.9\% of contracts converging within 1.0 percentage point absolute volatility error} ($|\Delta\sigma| \le 0.0100$, 100.0\% within 10 percentage points) and 97.6\% converging in exactly one Newton-Raphson pass (99.9\% within two passes). The $K/S \in [0.90, 1.10]$ sampling corresponds to $S/K \in [0.909, 1.111]$, placing all contracts within the liquid near-the-money regime where the Pad\'{e} logarithm approximation holds tightest.

To evaluate extreme dynamic range, a second 10,000-contract DPI-C co-simulation was executed over the extended domain $K/S \in [0.70, 1.40]$. In the liquid portion ($0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$, 4,394 contracts / 43.9\%), the engine achieves MAE of $8.80 \times 10^{-4}$ ($0.088\%$ vol, median $0.94$ vol-bps), with 98.8\% converging within 1.0 percentage point error. Across the 5,606 deep-wing contracts ($S/K < 0.85$ or $S/K > 1.15$, 56.1\%), where Vega diminishes and the 18-cycle CORDIC fallback engages, MAE is $0.0225$ (median $0.126\%$ vol), yielding an empirical blended MAE of $0.439(8.80 \times 10^{-4}) + 0.561(0.0225) = \mathbf{0.0130}$ ($1.30\%$ vol) with a global median error of $1.50$ vol-bps and strictly zero arithmetic overflow or divergence.

\textbf{Empirical SPX Market-Data Replay:} To evaluate numerical precision on empirical SPX market-price distributions and high-notional strikes, we evaluated a captured market snapshot of 1,382 active CBOE European SPX contracts (spot $S = \$7{,}591.70$, captured September 11, 2026; 15 expiries with $T \in [0.015, 1.0]$~yr, strikes \$5{,}000--\$8{,}000). Market quotes $C_{\text{mkt}}$ use bid-ask midpoints filtering crossed or sub-intrinsic values. Because hardware implements the canonical Black-Scholes formulation, this replay serves as a fixed-point precision and dynamic-range stress test under empirical pricing rather than a production dividend-aware IV study; reference IVs were computed via double-precision SciPy \texttt{brentq} ($xtol=10^{-8}$) on the identical canonical model. Input vectors were streamed through the RTL DPI-C path with bit-accurate fixed-point arithmetic identical to synthesized silicon. Across 989 liquid quotes ($0.95 \le S/K \le 1.05$), the engine achieves a median absolute error of \textbf{$1.64 \times 10^{-4}$ absolute volatility (1.64 vol-bps)}, with 54.3\% within $1.0 \times 10^{-3}$ ($<10$ vol-bps) and 66.3\% within $0.0100$ (1.0 percentage point). Across all 1,382 quotes up to \$8,000 strike, zero arithmetic overflow occurred, confirming native Q8.24 scale normalization ($\tilde{S}=S/K, \tilde{C}=C/K$) on \$7,500+ spot prices, while hardware clamping ensured 100\% numerical stability with zero divergence. A 5-case corner testbench ($T=0.001$~yr, zero rate, $\sigma=150\%$) confirmed safe clamping.

\subsection{First-Order Error-Budget Sanity Check}
The observed MAE of $1.57 \times 10^{-4}$ absolute volatility ($0.0157$ percentage points) is verified via a first-order error budget linking hardware approximations to volatility perturbation ($\Delta\sigma \approx \Delta C / \nu$):
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pad\'{e} Logarithm ($\Delta_{\ln}$):} For $x = S/K = 1+u$, [1/1] Pad\'{e} error is $\Delta_{\ln} \approx u^3/12 \le 3.6 \times 10^{-4}$ ($|u| \le 0.15$), inducing $\Delta\sigma_{\text{Pad\'{e}}} \approx 4.5 \times 10^{-5}$ ($0.0045\%$ vol).
    \item \textbf{Hyperbolic CORDIC ($e^{-rT}, e^{-d_1^2/2}$):} 16-stage CORDIC precision ($2^{-16}$) induces price error $\Delta C \approx 7.6 \times 10^{-6} K$. Dividing by typical Vega $\nu \approx 0.35 K$ yields $\Delta\sigma_{\text{CORDIC}} \approx 2.8 \times 10^{-5}$ ($0.0028\%$ vol).
    \item \textbf{CDF \& Quantization:} 5th-order Horner CDF ($|\epsilon| \le 7.5 \times 10^{-8}$) yields $\Delta\sigma_{\text{Horner}} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-6}$ ($0.00012\%$), while Q8.24 roundoff contributes RMS uncertainty $\approx 2.5 \times 10^{-6}$.
    \item \textbf{Price Convergence Termination:} With mean $S = \$50$ and $\bar{T} = 0.8$ ($\bar{\nu} \approx \$17.35$), mean termination error $\overline{|\Delta C|} \approx \$0.0025$ yields $\Delta\sigma_{\text{stop}} \approx \frac{\$0.0025}{\$17.35} \approx 1.45 \times 10^{-4}$ ($0.0145\%$ vol).
\end{itemize}
Root-sum-square of these components yields $\approx 1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ ($0.015\%$ vol), providing an order-of-magnitude sanity check consistent with the empirical DPI-C simulation MAE of $1.57 \times 10^{-4}$ across 10,000 contracts against double precision (non-independent error sources).

\subsection{Algorithmic Ablation: Seeding vs. Unseeded Newton-Raphson}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{With Analytical Seeding:} 97.6\% of options converge in 1 iteration, and 99.9\% in $\le 2$ iterations. Zero divergence occurs. Sustained throughput is 390.62~MOps/s.
    \item \textbf{Without Seeding ($\sigma_0 = 0.20$):} Contracts require an average of 4.8 iterations, and 4.2\% fail to converge or oscillate when $\nu < 0.02$. Seeding reduces average iteration count by 79\% (from 4.8 to 1.024 passes/contract).
\end{itemize}

We use the A100 only as a contextual forward-pricing reference rather than an equivalent IV+Greeks benchmark; even against this baseline, the FPGA provides superior energy efficiency while avoiding host-device transfers and kernel-batching mechanisms, maintaining deterministic hardware control flow.

\subsection{Deployment Considerations and Scaling Projection}
Deployment spans two tiers: (1)~\textit{Tier-1 Inline SmartNIC Filtering} ($<25$~W PCIe mezzanine), where our Artix-7 design consumes 4.258~W post-route estimated power (17\% of a 25~W budget) alongside 10GbE MACs with low thermal burden; and (2)~\textit{Tier-2 Centralized Co-Processing} (75--225~W datacenter accelerators).

To verify datacenter scalability, the 4-core engine was placed and routed on an AMD Alveo U50 card (\texttt{xcu50-fsvh2104-2-e}), closing timing at 250.00~MHz ($WNS = +0.073\text{ ns}$) and delivering 1.00~GOps/s at 9.560~W with strictly 0 BRAM and 0 URAM (occupying 12.13\% LUTs and 576 DSPs). Linear scaling projections indicate that 32 parallel cores on U50 would consume 4,608 DSPs (77.4\%) for a \textbf{projected 8.00~GOps/s} within an estimated $\sim$65~W board power (scaling dynamic power linearly while retaining static overhead, under the 75~W PCIe ceiling). On a VU9P (6,840 DSPs), 44 cores project to \textbf{11.00~GOps/s}. Crucially, on-chip BRAM/URAM blocks remain completely available for external 100GbE FIFOs and order-book tracking.

\section{Conclusion}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
We presented a four-core FPGA acceleration engine for real-time option implied volatility and concurrent Greeks ($\Delta, \nu, \Gamma$) calculation. Synergizing Brenner-Subrahmanyam guess seeding, Q8.24 scale-invariance normalization, and a zero-BRAM microarchitecture, the engine achieves physical timing closure at 100~MHz on Artix-7 200T (400~MOps/s, 4.258~W post-route power estimate) and 250~MHz on Alveo U50 (1.00~GOps/s, 9.56~W post-route estimate) with strictly 0 BRAM and 0 URAM. DPI-C co-simulation confirms high accuracy ($MAE = 1.57 \times 10^{-4}$ absolute volatility) with 99.9\% of contracts within 1.0 percentage point error. Delivering a peak throughput of 400~MOps/s, corresponding to 28.1$\times$ the companion AVX2 CPU throughput, the design demonstrates a viable, resource-efficient FPGA architecture for latency-sensitive streaming option pricing.
