\title{A Four-Core Zero-BRAM FPGA Accelerator for Implied-Volatility Solving and Concurrent Greek Evaluation}

\author{
\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Affiliation Withheld for Double-Blind Review}}
}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Streaming option analytics require repeated implied-volatility (IV) solving and risk sensitivity evaluation under low-latency and tight resource constraints. We present a four-core FPGA accelerator for Black-Scholes IV solving with concurrent Delta, Vega, and Gamma extraction. The design integrates a Brenner-Subrahmanyam analytical seed, a fully pipelined fixed-point Black-Scholes datapath, and distributed-LUTRAM/SRL context storage with no Block RAM or UltraRAM in the accelerator core. Implemented on AMD Artix-7 200T and Alveo U50, the configurations achieve nominal post-route timing closure at 100~MHz and 250~MHz, delivering nominal peak throughputs of 400~MOps/s and 1.00~GOps/s, corresponding to pass-adjusted nominal datapath throughputs of 151.82~MOps/s on Artix-7 and 379.57~MOps/s on U50 in the evaluated liquid regime ($\bar{P}=2.635$ passes, $3.04\times$ faster than fixed 8-pass at equivalent accuracy). In full-system co-simulation under a 16-credit/core testbench window, closed-loop credit-constrained streaming sustains 8.18~MOps/s with zero loss. 88.7\% of liquid options converge in $\le 3$ passes. For the liquid subset ($0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$, $N=4{,}394$), converged Greek MAEs are $4.38{\times}10^{-3}$ for Delta, 0.0963 for Vega, and $2.39{\times}10^{-4}$ for Gamma. Post-route power estimates are 4.258~W on Artix-7 and 9.560~W on U50 (10.65~nJ/op and 9.56~nJ/op). Eliminating 28 RAMB18E1 primitives (+857 LUTs, $+0.81\%$) achieves timing closure at 100~MHz (+0.109~ns WNS delta) and consumes 0 BRAM in the core, leaving all 365 BRAM36 blocks for system buffering. Replay on 1,382 CBOE SPX quotes validates dynamic-range normalization up to \$7,643 spot with zero arithmetic overflow observed. The architecture delivers cycle-deterministic per-pass execution (cold transaction latency is 230 AXI cycles / 227 core cycles; total latency is pass-dependent); PHY, MAC, and PCIe transfers remain outside the measurement boundary.
\end{abstract}

Iterative root-finding algorithms are notoriously difficult to pipeline on FPGAs~\cite{omahony2024role}: the number of loop iterations is data-dependent, transcendental function units are deeply pipelined (33--64 cycles), and in-flight intermediate results must be preserved across variable-length feedback paths. Black-Scholes implied volatility (IV) inversion is a canonical instance of this class~\cite{black1973pricing}. Given market price $C_\text{mkt}$ and contract parameters $\{S, K, T, r\}$, the IV solver inverts $C_\text{BS}(\sigma) = C_\text{mkt}$ via Newton-Raphson (NR), with each pass requiring evaluation of $\sqrt{T}$, $\ln(S/K)$, the CDF, and a division---all sharing deep pipeline resources across a variable number of feedback iterations. Motivated by streaming market-data and option-analytics workloads where market-making systems track IV and risk sensitivities (Delta $\Delta$, Gamma $\Gamma$, Vega $\nu$) across thousands of strikes and expiries~\cite{cboe2023specs}, conventional NR implementations face two hardware obstacles: (1)~\textit{Numerical sensitivity}---when Vega $\nu \to 0$ (short maturities or deep wings), unseeded iterations overshoot or diverge; and (2)~\textit{Non-deterministic latency}---CPU and GPU runtimes incur OS scheduling and PCIe queuing jitter that prevents cycle-deterministic streaming execution. To overcome these challenges, we present a zero-block-RAM FPGA engine for streaming IV solving with cycle-deterministic per-pass execution and concurrent Greeks extraction.

\subsection{Key Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Analytical Guess Seeder \& Dynamic Loopback:} Brenner-Subrahmanyam seeder achieves 4.80~vol-bps liquid MAE in an average of 2.635 passes, delivering 151.82~MOps/s pass-adjusted nominal datapath throughput ($3.04\times$ faster than fixed 8-pass at equivalent accuracy).
    \item \textbf{Zero-BRAM Context Architecture:} Replaces 28 RAMB18E1 context blocks with distributed LUTRAM/SRL32 primitives (+857 LUTs, $+0.81\%$), providing placement flexibility that achieves timing closure at 100.05~MHz (+0.109~ns WNS delta) while consuming 0 BRAM in the accelerator core, preserving all 365 BRAM36 blocks for network packet buffering.
    \item \textbf{Priority-Loopback Scheduler \& Headroom Invariant:} Enforces strict loopback priority and a 4-slot scoreboard headroom reservation ($N_{\text{active}} \ge 60$). Cycle-accurate stress testing up to 256-contract bursts observed zero deadlocks with RTL assertions confirming headroom invariance, while validating the idealized throughput model $T(p) = \frac{400}{1+p}$ under single-loopback assumptions.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic-Range Normalization \& Empirical Validation:} Spatial scaling across 1, 2, and 4 cores on Artix-7 maintains linear throughput scaling while documenting how increasing routing dominance (37.5\% to 61.4\%) reduces timing margin from 2.114~ns to 0.005~ns. Replay on 1,382 CBOE SPX quotes validates dynamic-range normalization up to \$7,643 spot with zero arithmetic overflow observed.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mathematical Formulation}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Black-Scholes Model and Option Greeks}
We evaluate European call options under standard Black-Scholes assumptions~\cite{black1973pricing}: continuous compounding at risk-free rate $r$, zero dividend yield ($q=0$), and constant volatility during contract inversion. For spot price $S$, strike $K$, and maturity $T$, the Black-Scholes price $C_{\text{BS}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{BS}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma) = S N(d_1) - K e^{-rT} N(d_2)
\end{equation}
where $N(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^{x} e^{-u^2/2} du$ is the cumulative standard normal distribution, and:
\begin{equation}
d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + \left(r + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)T}{\sigma \sqrt{T}}, \quad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma \sqrt{T}
\end{equation}
Note that $d_2$ is computed strictly as a subtraction $d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T}$, directly reusing intermediate $d_1$ without duplicating the 33-cycle divider.

The primary Greeks ($\Delta = \partial C/\partial S$, $\nu = \partial C/\partial \sigma$, $\Gamma = \partial^2 C/\partial S^2$) evaluate concurrently as:
\begin{equation}
\Delta = N(d_1), \quad \nu = S \sqrt{T} N'(d_1), \quad \Gamma = \frac{N'(d_1)}{S \sigma \sqrt{T}}
\end{equation}
where $N'(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-x^2/2}$ is the standard normal PDF.

\subsection{Dynamic-Range Normalization}
The engine operates in native Q8.24 fixed-point dollars ($S, K \le \$128.00$). Native prices ($S, K, C_{\text{mkt}}$) use unsigned Q8.24 ($[0, 255.999]$), allowing sum $(S+K)$ to evaluate up to $\$256$ in an internal 9-bit integer field (Q9.24) inside the adder before normalizing, while deviates and sensitivities ($d_1, d_2, \Delta, \sigma$) use signed Q8.24 with saturation logic. Transcendental inputs are scale-invariant: the Pad\'{e} ratio $(S - K)/(S + K) \equiv (S/K - 1)/(S/K + 1)$ is identical in dollars or normalized units, eliminating an ingress divider. Deviates $d_1, d_2$, discount $e^{-rT}$, and probabilities $N(d_1), N(d_2)$ are dimensionless. Thus, $C_{\text{BS}}$ and $\nu$ evaluate directly in dollars, checking $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| \le \$0.0100$ without denormalization penalty.

For high-notional index contracts (e.g., SPX at $\$5{,}000$), the architecture exploits Black-Scholes linear homogeneity to normalize high-notional contracts into the Q8.24 operating range: $C_{\text{BS}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma) = K \cdot C_{\text{BS}}(S/K, 1.0, T, r, \sigma)$. Pre-normalized inputs $(\tilde{S} = S/K, \tilde{K} = 1.0, \tilde{C}_{\text{mkt}} = C_{\text{mkt}}/K)$ enter the engine directly with threshold $\tilde{\epsilon} = \$0.01 / K$, mapping high-notional quotes into the compact Q8.24 dynamic range without datapath widening. Because price residual error and Vega scale proportionally under normalization ($\Delta\tilde{C} / \tilde{\nu} = (\Delta C / K) / (\nu / K) = \Delta C / \nu$), the Newton-Raphson update step $\Delta\sigma$ preserves exact mathematical scale invariance. Evaluated empirical replay demonstrates successful Q8.24 normalized evaluation on the evaluated \$7,500+ SPX quotes with zero arithmetic overflow observed. Throughout this paper, error is reported in volatility basis points: $1\text{ vol-bps} = 10^{-4}$ ($0.01\%$) in absolute volatility units ($|\sigma_{\text{calc}} - \sigma_{\text{ref}}|$). We quantify moneyness as $m = S/K$ (reciprocal $K/S = 1/m$). Synthetic evaluation vectors sample $K/S \in [0.70, 1.40]$ ($m \in [0.714, 1.429]$), while market trading regime classification adopts $m \in [0.85, 1.15]$ (approximately $K/S \in [0.87, 1.18]$) for liquid quotes, and $m < 0.85$ or $m > 1.15$ for deep wings; near-the-money specifically denotes $K/S \in [0.90, 1.10]$ ($m \in [0.909, 1.111]$).

\textit{Word-Length Precision Exploration:} We conduct a systematic word-length Pareto exploration across 24-bit to 48-bit fixed-point formats and 32-bit floating-point (detailed in Section~\ref{sec:eval_precision} and Fig.~\ref{fig:precision_pareto}). Across 10,000 contracts, Q8.24 emerges as the selected accuracy/resource design point: narrower formats (Q6.18, Q7.21) suffer catastrophic dynamic-range or polynomial truncation ($>1{,}000$~vol-bps MAE), while wider formats (Q9.27, Q12.36) and FP32 exceed Artix-7 200T DSP capacity ($>740$ DSPs, up to 155.7\% utilization) without meaningful accuracy gains under exchange residual stopping criteria.

\subsection{Analytical Guess Seeding (Brenner-Subrahmanyam)}
Standard Newton-Raphson update steps follow $\sigma_{n+1} = \sigma_n - [C_{\text{BS}}(\sigma_n) - C_{\text{mkt}}]/\nu(\sigma_n)$. Unseeded guesses (e.g., $\sigma_0 = 0.20$) require more iterations and can diverge when $\nu \to 0$. In contrast, the Brenner-Subrahmanyam approximation~\cite{brenner1988simple} requires only a scalar multiply and non-restoring divider while directly reusing the shared $\sqrt{T}$ unit. Alternatives like Corrado-Miller~\cite{corrado1996improved} require an extra square-root unit ($>30$ cycles), whereas rational expansions like J{\"a}ckel~\cite{jackel2015lets} consume dozens of DSPs. Replacing spot $S$ with symmetric mean $(S+K)/2$ prevents near-the-money bias:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_0 = \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\, C_{\text{mkt}}}{\sqrt{T} \cdot (S + K)/2} \approx \frac{2.5066\, C_{\text{mkt}}}{\sqrt{T} \cdot (S + K)/2}
\end{equation}
Because both $C_{\text{mkt}}$ and $(S+K)/2$ scale linearly with $K$, this ratio is scale-invariant under normalization. Note that $\sigma_0 \propto 1/\sqrt{T}$, evaluated directly with $\sqrt{T}$ in the denominator of the 33-cycle guess divider. In hardware (64 cycles), this closed-form seed provides an accurate initial point: across the liquid regime, relative seed error remains below 0.5\%, enabling 88.7\% of liquid contracts to converge in $\le 3$ passes (averaging $\bar{P} = 2.635$ passes per liquid contract, and 3.651 across the extended domain).

\subsection{High-Speed Polynomial and Elementary Function Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pad\'{e} Logarithm:} We evaluate the [1/1] Pad\'{e} approximant $\widehat{\ln}(x) = 2 \frac{x - 1}{x + 1}$ for $x = S/K$ (from $\ln x = 2\operatorname{atanh}(\frac{x-1}{x+1})$). The analytical error $\max_{x \in [0.85, 1.15]} |\ln(x) - \widehat{\ln}(x)| \le 3.6 \times 10^{-4}$ ($<0.22\%$ relative) is obtained by maximizing the closed-form remainder $R(u) \approx \frac{2}{3}u^3$ for $u = \frac{x-1}{x+1} \le 0.070$ in real arithmetic prior to quantization. For deep wings ($|S-K| > 0.15K$), the engine engages an 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm fallback (SRL15-padded to 33 cycles), preserving fixed datapath latency.
    \item \textbf{Horner Normal CDF:} $N(x)$ follows Abramowitz \& Stegun (7.1.26)~\cite{abramowitz1964handbook}: $N(x) = 1 - Z(x)\sum_{i=1}^5 b_i t^i$, where scale factor $t = 1/(1+p|x|)$ is evaluated by a dedicated 33-cycle non-restoring divider ($t_1, t_2$ for $d_1, d_2$), followed by 5th-order Horner single-DSP MAC stages. The theoretical error $|\epsilon| \le 7.5 \times 10^{-8}$ ($x \ge 0$) refers to the real-valued formulation before coefficient quantization; complete fixed-point error is evaluated in end-to-end DPI-C co-simulation. The pipeline applies symmetry $N(-x) = 1 - N(x)$, clamping to $[0, 1]$ with standard coefficients ($p=0.2316419$, $b_1 \dots b_5$~\cite{abramowitz1964handbook}).
    \item \textbf{Hyperbolic CORDIC:} Exponentials $e^{-rT}$ and $e^{-d_1^2/2}$ are computed using a fully pipelined 16-stage hyperbolic CORDIC engine.
\end{itemize}

\section{Hardware Microarchitecture}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} shows the top-level architecture, comprising four identical cores coordinated by round-robin ingress and an AXI4-Stream egress arbiter.

\subsection{Stage 1: Ingress Registration \& Queuing (1 Cycle)}
The ingress interface receives a 256-bit AXI4-Stream packet containing $\{S, K, T, r, C_{\text{mkt}}, \text{TID}\}$. Ingress values are registered in 1 clock cycle and tagged with a 6-bit TID. Normalization division is omitted; the Pad\'{e} ratio $\frac{S-K}{S+K} \equiv \frac{\tilde{S}-1}{\tilde{S}+1}$ is evaluated inside Stage 3, saving 32 cycles.

\subsection{Stage 2: Analytical Guess Seeder \& Shared Sqrt Unit (64 Cycles)}
The seeder evaluates Eq.~(4): $\sigma_0 = \frac{2.5066\, C_{\text{mkt}}}{\sqrt{T} \cdot (S + K)/2}$. A digit-recurrence unit computes $\sqrt{T}$ in 29 cycles (plus alignment), storing $\sqrt{T}$ in distributed context RAM and forwarding it downstream, eliminating a redundant square-root core. An internal non-restoring divider evaluates the ratio in 33 cycles, producing a valid $\sigma_0$ in exactly 64 cycles.

\subsection{Stage 3: Core Pipelined Black-Scholes Datapath (126 Cycles)}
The core Stage-3 datapath is a 126-cycle fully unrolled feedforward pipeline measured from input register to output register (including all delay-matching SRLs and CORDIC padding, verified in cycle-accurate RTL simulation), accepting one contract/cycle ($II = 1$). Sub-operations are partitioned across registered boundaries. For deep wings ($|S-K| > 0.15K$), the 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm is latency-matched via SRL15 to 33 cycles, maintaining a fixed 126-cycle latency for each Stage-3 pass.

\subsection{Zero-BRAM Context Memory Architecture}
Conventional designs map FIFOs to Block RAMs (RAMB18/36). Our engine implements all context stores ($64 \times 32$-bit) exclusively via distributed LUTRAM (\texttt{ram\_style = "distributed"}) and SRL32s, consuming 0 BRAMs/URAMs in the accelerator core. Feedforward state flows through SRL32s ($II=1$), while distributed stores retain static parameters $\{S, K, C_{\text{mkt}}, r, T\}$ across transaction lifetimes (227 core cycles cold / 230 AXI cycles, 386 loopback) to reinject data for NR updates. Each core provisions 64 physical context slots (256 slots total), tracked by \texttt{in\_flight\_count} and a 64-bit scoreboard (\texttt{tid\_busy\_mask}) asserting backpressure when in-flight contracts reach $\ge 60$ slots ($\ge 4$ headroom slots) or the requested local TID is active. Under this four-slot headroom admission policy, the four-core array admits up to $4 \times 60 = 240$ active in-flight transactions simultaneously. Because loopback retains its pre-allocated slot with strict priority over new ingress, re-entry cannot be blocked, preventing deadlock; new-ingress fairness is preserved by the 4-slot headroom policy. On Artix-7, this consumes 1,256 LUTRAM and 1,804 SRL32s within context stores (314 LUTRAM, 451 SRL32 per core), with 6,852 SRLs across the datapath for delay matching (8,656 total SRLs, 18.74\%); on Alveo U50, it occupies 0.28\% of LUTs, leaving all BRAM/URAM blocks unconstrained for external system buffering (PHY/MAC/PCIe outside boundary).

\textit{Controlled BRAM Ablation:} A matched Artix-7 implementation replacing the distributed context store with synchronous BRAM context memory produced bit-exact IV/Greek outputs for all 10,000 canonical contracts after collision-safe iteration-state arbitration (10,000/10,000 exact bit identity). The BRAM implementation consumed 28 RAMB18 blocks and exhibited a one-cycle higher AXI cold latency (231 vs. 230 cycles; 699.55 vs. 695.67 cycles mean transaction latency, Section~\ref{sec:bram_ablation}).

\subsection{Stage 4: Newton-Raphson Update Step \& Greek Dividers (33 Cycles)}
Following Stage 3, each core checks convergence $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| \le \epsilon$, where $\epsilon = 32'd167772 \approx \$0.0100$ in Q8.24, chosen as a representative \$0.01 exchange convention (price error scales as $\Delta\sigma \approx \Delta C / \nu$). Stage-4 latency (33 cycles) is measured from the Stage-3 output register to the loopback/egress decision register. A contract is classified as converged strictly when satisfying $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| \le \epsilon$; reaching the 8-pass ceiling without meeting this residual would be registered as a non-convergence failure. If $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| > \epsilon$, update $\Delta\sigma = (C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}})/\nu$ is evaluated by a 33-cycle divider (\texttt{u\_nr\_divider}) concurrently with the 33-cycle Gamma divider (\texttt{u\_gamma\_divider}). Hardware clamping prevents oscillation as $\nu \to 0$: NR step bounded by $|\Delta\sigma| \le 0.25$ (\texttt{MAX\_STEP\_Q24}), volatility clamped to $[0.01, 5.00]$, seed clamped to $[0.05, 3.00]$, and \texttt{u\_nr\_divider} saturates upon zero denominator. Refined $\sigma_{n+1}$ loops back with priority via the active TID scoreboard. Because Greeks are registered to egress solely upon convergence, two-pass contracts re-evaluate $\Delta, \nu, \Gamma$ at $\sigma_{n+1}$, ensuring exported sensitivities strictly match final converged IV.

\subsection{Stage 5: Multi-Core Arbitration \& 128-Bit Egress (2 Cycles)}
Upon convergence, results are packed into a 128-bit data payload: $\text{Egress}[127:0] = \{ \text{TID}[5:0],\, \Gamma[25:0],\, \nu[31:0],\, \Delta[31:0],\, \sigma[31:0] \}$, carrying the 6-bit local TID, 26-bit Gamma, 32-bit Vega, 32-bit Delta, and 32-bit IV ($6+26+32+32+32=128$ bits). The 2-bit originating core ID (\texttt{active\_rd[1:0]} in \texttt{iv\_multi\_engine\_top.sv}) is retained at egress as an interface sideband/metadata signal. The array-wide composite global tag is $\{\text{Core}[1:0], \text{TID}[5:0]\}$ (8 bits), uniquely distinguishing up to 240 admitted in-flight transactions across the 256 provisioned slots without a reorder buffer. Egress words are emitted in work-conserving arbitration order. Each core enforces local TID uniqueness across its 64 slots via \texttt{tid\_busy\_mask[63:0]}. $\Gamma$ occupies 26 bits (signed Q2.24, $[-2.0, +1.999]$) by truncating 6 redundant sign bits from Q8.24; while bounded by $\Gamma \le 1/(S\sigma\sqrt{2\pi T})$, this packing is empirically confirmed without saturation across all 113,882 evaluated transactions in the full-system validation suite (Datasets A--D, max: 0.2357; formal range safety across all theoretically conceivable $\{S,T,\sigma\}$ is not claimed). Extreme inputs are protected by hardware clamping at the divider output to prevent arithmetic wraparound. A 4-way arbiter drains output FIFOs in 2 cycles.

\subsection{Pipeline Latency and Sustained Throughput Reconciliation}
We distinguish between four operational terms: a \textbf{contract} denotes one input quote; a \textbf{pass} denotes one evaluation through the Stage-3/Stage-4 datapath; an \textbf{iteration} denotes an unseeded Newton-Raphson feedback cycle; and an \textbf{operation (Op)} denotes one fully converged contract producing final IV and all three Greeks ($\Delta, \nu, \Gamma$). Stage-3 $II=1$ describes feedforward acceptance, while sustained operation throughput reflects completed contracts adjusted by average passes per contract. The internal cold datapath requires 227 cycles ($L_{\text{core}} = 1\,(\text{Stg 1}) + 64\,(\text{Stg 2}) + 1\,(\text{Q}) + 126\,(\text{Stg 3}) + 33\,(\text{Stg 4}) + 2\,(\text{Stg 5}) = \mathbf{227\text{ cycles}}$; 2.27~$\mu$s on Artix-7 at 100~MHz / 0.908~$\mu$s on U50 at 250~MHz; isolated Stage-3 core datapath is 126~cycles, 1.26~$\mu$s / 0.504~$\mu$s), while the complete end-to-end AXI transaction measured from input acceptance to output handshake requires $\mathbf{230\text{ cycles}}$ (2.30~$\mu$s on Artix-7 / 0.920~$\mu$s on U50), including 3 interface handshake and FIFO synchronization cycles. Loopback bypasses Stages~1--2, requiring Stages~3--4 ($126 + 33 = \mathbf{159\text{ cycles}}$, 1.59~$\mu$s / 0.636~$\mu$s), yielding 386 core cycles for two passes (3.86~$\mu$s / 1.544~$\mu$s; 389 AXI cycles total) and $227 + (P-1)\times 159$ core cycles for $P$ passes (total AXI transaction latency $230 + (P-1)\times 159 + \delta_{\text{queue}}$). Loopback does not stall the Stage-3 $II=1$ datapath; each loopback consumes one ingress scheduling opportunity with strict priority over incoming traffic. Pass-adjusted nominal datapath throughput is derived from the $II=1$ peak ingress rate divided by the empirical average number of passes per contract ($T_{\text{nominal}} = T_{\text{peak}} / \bar{P}$); it represents open-loop datapath service capacity and is distinct from closed-loop credit-constrained RTL co-simulation throughput (8.18~MOps/s under a 16-credit testbench window, Table~\ref{tab:saturation_diagnostics}). On Alveo U50, pass-adjusted nominal rates (379.57~MOps/s liquid) reflect projected nominal datapath capacity at 250~MHz, not a physical co-simulation run.

\textbf{Liquid Trading ($m \in [0.85, 1.15]$):} Across 4,394 liquid contracts in bit-accurate Q8.24 evaluation, 88.7\% converge within $\le 3$ passes (Pass 2: 49.5\%, Pass 3: 38.8\%, Pass 4: 10.1\%, Pass 5--8: 1.2\%), with an average of $\bar{P} = 2.635$ passes per contract. Across 4 cores at 100~MHz, pass-adjusted nominal datapath throughput is $T_{\text{nominal, liquid}} = \frac{400.00\text{ MOps/s}}{2.635} = \mathbf{151.82\text{ MOps/s}}$ at an IV MAE of 4.80~vol-bps (379.57~MOps/s on Alveo U50). This achieves a $3.04\times$ speedup over fixed 8-pass (50.00~MOps/s, 4.50~vol-bps) while matching its accuracy within 0.30~vol-bps.

\subsection{Zero-BRAM Context Signoff Comparison}
\label{sec:bram_ablation}
To quantify the benefit of the Zero-BRAM microarchitecture, we synthesized and implemented an identical four-core baseline using dedicated Block RAM primitives (28 RAMB18E1, equivalent to 14 BRAM36 blocks) for context storage. Under identical placement seeds and timing constraints (100~MHz, Artix-7 200T), the BRAM baseline violates timing with $WNS = -0.104\text{ ns}$ (14 failing endpoints, derived $F_{\max} = 98.97\text{ MHz}$) and incurs 1 additional cycle of cold latency (228 vs. 227 core cycles; 231 vs. 230 AXI cycles) due to synchronous BRAM context fetching. The BRAM-based implementation exhibits a routing-dominated critical path, with routing accounting for 59.2\% of the reported critical-path delay. The distributed implementation provides greater placement flexibility for context storage and achieves timing closure at 100~MHz ($WNS = +0.005\text{ ns}$, derived $F_{\max} = 100.05\text{ MHz}$, MET, a $+0.109\text{ ns}$ WNS gain) at a minor cost of $+857$ Slice LUTs ($+0.81\%$, from 105,441 to 106,298 LUTs), while consuming 0 BRAM in the accelerator core and leaving all 365 BRAM36 blocks available for system buffering. Bit-level output comparison confirms 100.0\% exact bit identity (10,000 / 10,000 contracts) across all evaluated options, including liquid, near-the-money, and extreme deep-wing regimes ($\max |\Delta\sigma| = 0.00000000$; $\Delta$, $\nu$, $\Gamma$ bit-identical). Decoupling iteration count tracking (\texttt{ctx\_iter}) into independent flip-flop registers with collision-safe dual-event commit ensures that simultaneous ingress initialization and loopback completion commit concurrently without write-port arbitration hazards, establishing empirical bit-exact equivalence across all 10,000 evaluated contracts between Zero-BRAM and BRAM baselines. Across all 10,000 canonical contracts, 0 lost, 0 duplicates, and 0 mismatches were observed ($N_{\text{acc}} = N_{\text{ret}} = 10{,}000$).

\subsection{Sub-Module Hardware Resource Breakdown}
Table~\ref{tab:submodule} summarizes post-implementation resource distribution per core. The seven pipelined non-restoring 33-cycle Q8.24 dividers per core---comprising (1) analytical guess ratio $\frac{C_\text{mkt}}{(S+K)/2}$, (2) Pad\'{e} logarithm argument $\frac{S-K}{S+K}$, (3) $d_1$ normal deviate, (4) $N(d_1)$ CDF scale parameter $t_1 = 1/(1+p|d_1|)$, (5) $N(d_2)$ CDF scale parameter $t_2 = 1/(1+p|d_2|)$, (6) Newton-Raphson update step $\Delta\sigma = (C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}})/\nu$, and (7) Gamma quotient $\Gamma = N'(d_1)/(S\sigma\sqrt{T})$---are implemented in distributed slice logic, consuming 0 DSPs. The 608 DSPs are distributed across the Horner normal CDF MAC chains (384), Black-Scholes datapath multipliers (176), the square-root/seeder unit (32), and CORDIC scaling stages (16). Sub-module logic scales symmetrically across cores ($\approx 26.6\text{k}$ LUTs and 152 DSPs/core), with 2,164 total SRLs per core (451 in context store, 1,713 for delay matching; 8,656 SRLs across 4 cores), bringing chip utilization to 106,298 Slice LUTs (79.45\%) and 608 DSPs (82.16\%) in Table~\ref{tab:util}.

\subsection{Deployment Considerations and Scaling Projection}
Deployment spans two tiers: (1)~\textit{Tier-1 Edge / Low-Power Filtering} ($<$25~W mezzanine), where Artix-7 consumes 4.258~W post-route estimated power, leaving BRAM available for MAC/DMA; and (2)~\textit{Tier-2 Centralized Co-Processing} (75--225~W). On Alveo U50, the 4-core engine delivers 1.00~GOps/s peak (379.57~MOps/s pass-adjusted nominal liquid) at 9.560~W with 0 BRAM/URAM (12.13\% LUTs, 576 DSPs). A linear 32-core scaling projection (4,608 DSPs, 77.4\% for 8.00~GOps/s peak within $\sim$65~W) is provided only as future capacity analysis and is not included in the measured results. The zero-BRAM core leaves dedicated on-chip block memory available for future co-location of network buffering or order-book tracking; such system integration and physical host interfaces remain outside the core measurement boundary.

\subsection{End-to-End Four-Core RTL Validation \& System Saturation}
To bridge physical FPGA implementation with algorithmic correctness under continuous traffic, the complete four-core RTL array was validated in an end-to-end DPI-C co-simulation suite across five structured phases:
(1)~\textbf{Diverse Dataset Validation (Phase 7A):} As detailed in Table~\ref{tab:rtl_validation}, the synthesized four-core RTL was evaluated across four diverse workloads totaling 113,882 transactions: Canonical ($N=10\text{k}$), Randomized Stability ($N=100\text{k}$), Boundary Stress Grid ($N=2.5\text{k}$), and CBOE SPX quotes ($N=1{,}382$). Across all 113,882 transactions, strict transaction integrity is maintained with zero lost contracts ($N_{\text{lost}}=0$), zero duplicate egress packets ($N_{\text{dup}}=0$), and zero TID routing mismatches, confirming that dynamic arbitration and distributed context stores preserve strict transaction-to-core mapping. In Dataset A, liquid IV MAE is 7.67~vol-bps (median 0.91~vol-bps; NTM MAE 2.01~vol-bps, median 0.96~vol-bps), reconciling with the 4.80~vol-bps post-update and 5.70~vol-bps pre-update algorithmic models due to fixed 33-cycle integer non-restoring divider and 16-stage CORDIC quantization in the full RTL synthesis model. On SPX index quotes (Dataset D), decomposing moneyness reveals 437.93~vol-bps MAE (78.88~vol-bps median) for near-the-money quotes ($0.95 \le S/K \le 1.05$, $N=1{,}175$) with 72.1\% single-pass convergence (230 cycles), 487.62~vol-bps MAE (122.84~vol-bps median) for liquid quotes ($0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$, $N=1{,}367$), and 331.52~vol-bps MAE (247.01~vol-bps median) for deep wings ($N=15$), validating dynamic-range normalization with zero arithmetic overflow observed.
(2)~\textbf{Randomized Long-Run Stability (Phase 7B):} Sustained line-rate injection of 100,000 independent contracts with randomized TIDs $\in [0, 63]$ ran continuously for 1,170,136 clock cycles (11.70~ms simulated time at 100~MHz) without pipeline stalls or scoreboard corruption, achieving 8.55~MOps/s sustained throughput with 14.84~vol-bps liquid IV MAE (1.81~vol-bps median) and concurrent Greek MAEs of $\Delta = 0.0112$, $\nu = 0.6359$, and $\Gamma = 0.0032$.
(3)~\textbf{Full-System Saturation \& In-Flight Headroom (Phase 7C):} Table~\ref{tab:saturation_diagnostics} reports system saturation diagnostics. We clearly distinguish three throughput definitions: (i)~peak pipeline ingress capacity of 400.00~MOps/s ($II=1$ at 100~MHz across 4 cores), (ii)~open-loop datapath service rate of 151.82~MOps/s for liquid traffic ($400 / 2.635$ passes), and (iii)~closed-loop credit-constrained co-simulation throughput of 8.14~MOps/s. By Little's Law ($T = N / L$), with a testbench credit pool of 16 in-flight credits per core ($N=64$ credits array-wide) and mean retirement latency $L = 698.4$ cycles ($6.984\,\mu\text{s}$ at 100~MHz), the credit-window theoretical upper bound is $64 / 6.984\,\mu\text{s} \approx 9.16$~MOps/s (measured 8.14~MOps/s under dynamic loopback preemption at an 8.14\% input acceptance duty cycle). Headline cold latency is exactly 230 clock cycles (2.30~$\mu$s; cycle of accepted AXI input to cycle of corresponding AXI output handshake, comprising 227 core datapath cycles $+ 3$ AXI interface/FIFO cycles). In-flight scoreboard context occupancy peaked at exactly 16 contracts per core (64 array-wide), strictly bounded by the testbench credit window, safely preserving the 4-slot hardware headroom invariant ($N_{\text{active}} \le 60$) and precluding deadlock.
(4)~\textbf{Downstream Backpressure Robustness (Phase 7D):} To evaluate egress resilience, the 10,000-contract stream was injected under 0\%, 20\%, and 50\% randomized \texttt{m\_axis\_tready} stalls. The 64-entry distributed output FIFO absorbs downstream stalls, keeping sustained throughput virtually unchanged ($<0.2\%$ variation: 8.14~MOps/s at 0\% and 20\%, 8.13~MOps/s at 50\%), while mean latency increases by only 1.35 cycles (698.37 to 699.72 cycles) with zero dropped packets and zero buffer overflows.
(5)~\textbf{Saturated BRAM Ablation (Phase 7E / Exp 1B):} Replaying identical canonical contracts through the BRAM baseline confirms 100.0\% exact bit-level output identity across all evaluated transactions (10,000 / 10,000 contracts) for implied volatility ($\max |\Delta\sigma| = 0.00000000$) and all extracted Greeks ($\Delta, \nu, \Gamma$). Decoupling \texttt{ctx\_iter} into dedicated register storage provides collision-safe dual-event commit, completely eliminating write-port arbitration hazards between concurrent ingress and loopback updates. Under saturated closed-loop traffic, Zero-BRAM delivers closed-loop throughput of 8.18 vs. 8.14~MOps/s (+0.04~MOps/s, +0.49\%) and a 3.88-cycle lower mean transaction latency (695.67 vs. 699.55 cycles; cold latency 230 vs. 231 cycles) by eliminating synchronous BRAM context-read latency bubbles that otherwise accumulate across multi-pass recirculations, with zero lost or duplicated contracts.

\section{Conclusion}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
We presented a four-core FPGA accelerator for streaming Black-Scholes IV solving and concurrent Greeks ($\Delta, \nu, \Gamma$). Synergizing Brenner-Subrahmanyam seeding, Q8.24 dynamic-range normalization via scale invariance, and a zero-BRAM microarchitecture, the engine achieves nominal post-route timing closure at 100~MHz on Artix-7 200T (400~MOps/s peak, 4.258~W) and 250~MHz on Alveo U50 (1.00~GOps/s peak, 9.560~W) with 0 BRAM/URAM in the accelerator core. In bit-accurate Q8.24 evaluation on the canonical benchmark, the dynamic loopback engine converges in an average of 2.635 passes, delivering a pass-adjusted nominal datapath throughput of 151.82~MOps/s on Artix-7 (379.57~MOps/s on U50; 8.18~MOps/s closed-loop co-simulation) at 4.80~vol-bps MAE ($3.04\times$ faster than fixed 8-pass at equivalent accuracy). For the evaluated near-the-money suite, 99.9\% of contracts were within 1.0 percentage point of reference IV ($|\Delta\sigma| \le 0.0100$). Replay on 1,382 CBOE SPX quotes validates dynamic-range normalization up to \$7,643 spot with zero arithmetic overflow observed. The design provides cycle-deterministic per-pass execution (end-to-end AXI cold latency is 0.92~$\mu$s on U50 / 2.30~$\mu$s on Artix-7, 227 core cycles; total transaction latency is pass-dependent), achieving 10.7$\times$ higher pass-adjusted liquid throughput (28.1$\times$ peak) and a 112.8$\times$ throughput-to-power ratio (297$\times$ peak) relative to a 16-thread AVX2 host CPU baseline under post-route models.

\noindent\textbf{Open-Source Artifact Reproducibility:} SystemVerilog RTL, Vivado implementation scripts for Artix-7 and Alveo U50, bit-accurate DPI-C co-simulation testbenches, and empirical CBOE SPX replay vectors will be made openly available upon publication at an anonymized repository.
