\title{A Zero-BRAM FPGA Architecture for Deterministic Low-Latency Implied-Volatility Solving}

\author{
\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Authors}
\IEEEauthorblockA{\textit{Affiliation Withheld for Double-Blind Review}}
}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Inverting the Black-Scholes pricing equation for implied volatility (IV) is a variable-iteration Newton-Raphson root-finding problem that is challenging to pipeline deterministically on an FPGA: each pass reuses transcendental sub-results ($\sqrt{T}$, $\ln(S/K)$, CDF), yet the number of passes is data-dependent and unknown at ingress. This paper presents a four-core, fully pipelined FPGA architecture that resolves this challenge through a priority-loopback reinjection scheme with a TID scoreboard, eliminating any reorder buffer while preserving $II{=}1$ datapath throughput. All per-contract context ($S,K,T,r,C_\text{mkt}$) is held exclusively in distributed LUTRAM and SRL32 shift registers, consuming strictly \textbf{zero Block RAMs and zero UltraRAMs}, leaving all dedicated on-chip memory available for potential external integration. Concurrent $\Delta$, $\nu$, and $\Gamma$ are extracted from shared intermediates without increasing the reported core pipeline depth. The four-core Artix-7 implementation reaches 400~MOps/s peak throughput and 390.62~MOps/s net liquid-regime throughput (245.8~MOps/s blended extended-domain) at 100~MHz (4.258~W post-route power estimate, $WNS{=}{+}0.005\text{ ns}$); on Alveo U50, it delivers 1.00~GOps/s peak (976.6~MOps/s liquid net) at 250~MHz ($WNS{=}{+}0.073\text{ ns}$, 0 BRAM, 0 URAM). DPI-C co-simulation over 10,000 synthetic contracts reports an IV MAE of $1.57{\times}10^{-4}$ (1.57~vol-bps) in the liquid near-the-money regime, with 99.9\% of contracts within 1.0 percentage point error, and $0.0130$ across the extended domain. In the liquid regime, concurrently extracted Greek MAEs are $4.38{\times}10^{-3}$ for Delta, $0.0963$ for Vega, and $2.39{\times}10^{-4}$ for Gamma. All results are post-route and DPI-C co-simulation; board-level PHY, MAC, and host PCIe transfers are outside the measurement boundary.
\end{abstract}

Iterative root-finding algorithms are notoriously difficult to pipeline on FPGAs~\cite{omahony2024role}: the number of loop iterations is data-dependent, transcendental function units are deeply pipelined (33--64 cycles), and in-flight intermediate results must be preserved across variable-length feedback paths. Black-Scholes implied volatility (IV) inversion is a canonical instance of this class~\cite{black1973pricing}. Given market price $C_\text{mkt}$ and contract parameters $\{S, K, T, r\}$, the IV solver inverts $C_\text{BS}(\sigma) = C_\text{mkt}$ via Newton-Raphson (NR), with each pass requiring evaluation of $\sqrt{T}$, $\ln(S/K)$, the CDF, and a division—all sharing deep pipeline resources across a variable number of feedback iterations. Options exchanges require IV and risk sensitivities (Delta $\Delta$, Gamma $\Gamma$, Vega $\nu$) on every price tick across thousands of strikes and expiries~\cite{cboe2023specs}. Conventional NR implementations face two hardware obstacles: (1)~\textit{Numerical sensitivity}—when Vega $\nu \to 0$ (short maturities or deep wings), unseeded iterations overshoot or diverge; and (2)~\textit{Non-deterministic latency}—CPU and GPU runtimes incur OS scheduling and PCIe queuing jitter that prevents cycle-deterministic streaming execution. To overcome these challenges, we present a deterministic, zero-block-RAM FPGA engine for streaming IV solving with concurrent Greeks extraction.

\subsection{Key Contributions}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Analytical Guess Engine:} Brenner-Subrahmanyam seeder is tailored for low relative error in the liquid near-the-money regime, enabling 97.6\% 1-pass convergence while forwarding shared $\sqrt{T}$.
    \item \textbf{Scale-Invariant Normalization:} Exploiting Black-Scholes linear homogeneity ($\tilde{S} = S/K, \tilde{K} = 1.0, \tilde{C} = C/K$) bounds dynamic range strictly within Q8.24, supporting high-notional contracts without multi-precision arithmetic.
    \item \textbf{Zero-BRAM Microarchitecture:} Context buffers map to distributed LUTRAM/SRL32 registers (\texttt{ram\_style = "distributed"}), consuming 0 BRAMs and leaving dedicated on-chip block RAMs completely available for external packet and DMA buffers.
    \item \textbf{Dual-Platform Timing Signoff:} Implemented on Artix-7 200T at 100~MHz (400~MOps/s, 4.258~W post-route power estimate, $WNS=+0.005\text{ ns}$) and Alveo U50 at 250~MHz (1.00~GOps/s, 9.560~W post-route estimate, $WNS=+0.073\text{ ns}$) with 0 BRAM/URAM. DPI-C co-simulation confirms $1.57 \times 10^{-4}$ absolute volatility MAE, yielding a peak throughput of 400~MOps/s (28.1$\times$ the companion AVX2 CPU throughput).
\end{itemize}

\section{Mathematical Formulation}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Black-Scholes Model and Option Greeks}
For a European call option with spot price $S$, strike $K$, maturity $T$, and risk-free rate $r$, the Black-Scholes price $C_{\text{BS}}$ is:
\begin{equation}
C_{\text{BS}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma) = S N(d_1) - K e^{-rT} N(d_2)
\end{equation}
where $N(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \int_{-\infty}^{x} e^{-u^2/2} du$ is the cumulative standard normal distribution, and:
\begin{equation}
d_1 = \frac{\ln(S/K) + \left(r + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)T}{\sigma \sqrt{T}}, \quad d_2 = d_1 - \sigma \sqrt{T}
\end{equation}
Note that $d_2$ is computed strictly as a subtraction $d_1 - \sigma\sqrt{T}$, directly reusing intermediate $d_1$ without duplicating the 33-cycle divider. The primary Greeks ($\Delta = \partial C/\partial S$, $\nu = \partial C/\partial \sigma$, $\Gamma = \partial^2 C/\partial S^2$) evaluate concurrently as:
\begin{equation}
\Delta = N(d_1), \quad \nu = S \sqrt{T} N'(d_1), \quad \Gamma = \frac{N'(d_1)}{S \sigma \sqrt{T}}
\end{equation}
where $N'(x) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} e^{-x^2/2}$ is the standard normal PDF.

\subsection{Scale-Invariance Normalization}
The engine operates in native Q8.24 fixed-point dollars ($S, K \le \$128.00$). Native prices ($S, K, C_{\text{mkt}}$) use unsigned Q8.24 ($[0, 255.999]$), allowing sum $(S+K)$ to evaluate up to $\$256$ in an internal 9-bit integer field (Q9.24) inside the adder before normalizing, while deviates and sensitivities ($d_1, d_2, \Delta, \sigma$) use signed Q8.24 with saturation logic. Transcendental inputs are scale-invariant: the Pad\'{e} ratio $(S - K)/(S + K) \equiv (S/K - 1)/(S/K + 1)$ is identical in dollars or normalized units, eliminating an ingress divider. Deviates $d_1, d_2$, discount $e^{-rT}$, and probabilities $N(d_1), N(d_2)$ are dimensionless. Thus, $C_{\text{BS}}$ and $\nu$ evaluate directly in dollars, checking $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| \le \$0.0100$ without denormalization penalty.

For high-notional index contracts (e.g., SPX at $\$5{,}000$), we exploit linear homogeneity: $C_{\text{BS}}(S, K, T, r, \sigma) = K \cdot C_{\text{BS}}(S/K, 1.0, T, r, \sigma)$. Pre-normalized inputs $(\tilde{S} = S/K, \tilde{K} = 1.0, \tilde{C}_{\text{mkt}} = C_{\text{mkt}}/K)$ enter the engine directly with threshold $\tilde{\epsilon} = \$0.01 / K$, bounding the principal normalized price variables to a compact Q8.24 range without datapath widening.

\textit{Word-Length Pareto Exploration:} Empirical precision analysis across 5,000 contracts confirms Q8.24 as the Pareto-optimal representation: narrower 24-bit Q6.18 fails (44.6\% volatility MAE) due to precision underflow in near-zero Vega divisions, while wider 48-bit Q12.36 doubles LUT footprint (1,711 to 3,240 LUTs) and reduces maximum operating frequency $F_{\max}$ from 250 to 185~MHz due to carry-chain depth with zero accuracy gain under the \$0.01 convergence residual. Emulated single-precision FP32 requires $2.8\times$ higher logic LUT density and 16 DSPs/core with non-deterministic rounding latency.

\subsection{Analytical Guess Seeding (Brenner-Subrahmanyam)}
Standard Newton-Raphson update steps follow $\sigma_{n+1} = \sigma_n - [C_{\text{BS}}(\sigma_n) - C_{\text{mkt}}]/\nu(\sigma_n)$. Unseeded guesses (e.g., $\sigma_0 = 0.20$) can require substantially more iterations and exhibit oscillation or divergence when Vega $\nu \to 0$. In contrast, the Brenner-Subrahmanyam approximation~\cite{brenner1988simple} is hardware-optimal, requiring only a scalar multiply and non-restoring divider while directly reusing the shared $\sqrt{T}$ unit. Alternatives like Corrado-Miller~\cite{corrado1996improved} require an extra square-root unit ($>30$ cycles), whereas rational expansions like J{\"a}ckel~\cite{jackel2015lets} consume dozens of DSPs with high-order polynomials and branch tables. Replacing spot $S$ with symmetric spot-strike mean $(S+K)/2$ prevents near-the-money bias:
\begin{equation}
\sigma_0 = \frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{\sqrt{T}} \cdot \frac{C_{\text{mkt}}}{(S + K)/2} \approx \frac{2.5066}{\sqrt{T}} \cdot \frac{C_{\text{mkt}}}{(S + K)/2}
\end{equation}
Evaluated in dedicated hardware in 64 clock cycles, this closed-form seed provides an accurate starting point: in the evaluated liquid regime, the seed exhibited relative error below 0.5\%; in the evaluated wing samples, the observed absolute seed error was below 0.08~vol. This empirically stable initialization directly enables single-pass convergence for 97.6\% of liquid quotes (deep wings average 2.1 passes).

\subsection{High-Speed Polynomial and Elementary Function Evaluation}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Pad\'{e} Logarithm:} We evaluate the [1/1] Pad\'{e} approximant $\ln(x) \approx 2 \frac{x - 1}{x + 1}$ for $x = S/K$ (from $\ln x = 2\operatorname{atanh}(\frac{x-1}{x+1})$). Absolute error is bounded by $3.6 \times 10^{-4}$ ($<0.22\%$ relative) across $0.85 \le S/K \le 1.15$, and $<0.05\%$ across $0.925 \le S/K \le 1.08$, capturing $>95\%$ of liquid quotes. For deep wings ($|S-K|/K > 0.15$), the engine engages an 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm fallback, latency-matched via SRL15 to 33 cycles, maintaining 126-cycle datapath determinism.
    \item \textbf{Horner Normal CDF:} $N(x)$ is computed via a 5th-order rational polynomial evaluated with Horner's scheme, pipelined into single-DSP multiply-accumulate stages~\cite{abramowitz1964handbook}.
    \item \textbf{Hyperbolic CORDIC:} Exponentials $e^{-rT}$ and $e^{-d_1^2/2}$ are computed using a fully pipelined 16-stage hyperbolic CORDIC engine.
\end{itemize}

\section{Hardware Microarchitecture}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
Fig.~\ref{fig:arch} shows the top-level architecture, comprising four identical cores coordinated by round-robin ingress and an AXI4-Stream egress arbiter.

\subsection{Stage 1: Ingress Registration \& Queuing (1 Cycle)}
The ingress interface receives a 256-bit AXI4-Stream packet containing $\{S, K, T, r, C_{\text{mkt}}, \text{TID}\}$. Ingress values are registered in 1 clock cycle and tagged with a 6-bit TID. Normalization division is omitted; the Pad\'{e} ratio $\frac{S-K}{S+K} \equiv \frac{\tilde{S}-1}{\tilde{S}+1}$ is evaluated inside Stage 3, saving 32 cycles.

\subsection{Stage 2: Analytical Guess Seeder \& Shared Sqrt Unit (64 Cycles)}
The seeder evaluates Eq.~(4): $\sigma_0 = \frac{2.5066}{\sqrt{T}} \frac{C_{\text{mkt}}}{(S + K)/2}$. A digit-recurrence unit computes $\sqrt{T}$ in 29 cycles (plus alignment), storing $\sqrt{T}$ in distributed context RAM and forwarding it downstream, eliminating a redundant square-root core. An internal non-restoring divider evaluates the ratio in 33 cycles, producing a valid $\sigma_0$ in exactly 64 cycles.

\subsection{Stage 3: Core Pipelined Black-Scholes Datapath (126 Cycles)}
The core datapath is a 126-cycle fully unrolled feedforward pipeline with Initiation Interval $II = 1$ (accepting one contract/cycle). Sub-operations are partitioned across registered boundaries. For deep wings ($|S-K|/K > 0.15$), the 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm is latency-matched via SRL15 to 33 cycles, maintaining 126-cycle deterministic latency across 100\% of transactions.

\subsection{Zero-BRAM Context Memory Architecture}
Conventional designs map FIFOs to Block RAMs (RAMB18/36), which are critical for 10GbE MAC and PCIe DMA rings. Our engine implements all context memories ($64 \times 32$-bit) exclusively using distributed LUTRAM (\texttt{ram\_style = "distributed"}) and SRL32 registers, consuming strictly \textbf{zero} BRAMs and \textbf{zero} UltraRAMs. Feedforward pipeline state flows through SRL32s ($II=1$), while distributed context stores retain static parameters $\{S, K, C_{\text{mkt}}, r, T\}$ across transaction lifetimes (227 cycles cold, 386 loopback) solely to reinject market data for NR updates. A context slot remains allocated from ingress until egress retirement. Each core maintains an independent 64-slot store ($4 \times 64 = 256$ slots), governed by an active \texttt{in\_flight\_count} counter that asserts ingress backpressure (\texttt{s\_axis\_tready = 0}) when in-flight contracts reach 63 of 64 slots. Because loopback reinjection has strict priority over new ingress, and at least one context slot is guaranteed reserved, loopback contracts never encounter a stalled or full buffer, mathematically preventing pipeline deadlock and ensuring every in-flight contract retires in bounded cycles without starvation. On Artix-7, this consumes 1,256 LUTRAM and 1,804 SRL32s within the context memories (314 LUTRAM and 451 SRL32 per core), with an additional 6,852 SRLs inferred across the datapath for feedforward delay matching (8,656 total SRLs, 18.74\%); on 16nm Alveo U50, it occupies merely 0.28\% of LUT capacity, leaving all dedicated BRAM/URAM blocks available for potential external packet buffers or host DMA integration (PHY/MAC/PCIe are outside the present measurement boundary).

\subsection{Stage 4: Newton-Raphson Update Step \& Greek Dividers (33 Cycles)}
Following Stage 3, each core checks convergence $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| \le \epsilon$, where $\epsilon = 32'd167772 \approx \$0.0100$ in Q8.24, chosen as a representative \$0.01 price-residual hardware convergence criterion. Note that this price-residual stopping criterion is a standard exchange quoting convention rather than a uniform implied-volatility accuracy guarantee, because the mapping from price error to volatility error scales inversely with Vega ($\Delta\sigma \approx \Delta C / \nu$). The reported volatility-error statistics therefore represent empirical measurements of the complete fixed-point solver, rather than a formal bound implied solely by the price residual threshold. If $|C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}}| > \epsilon$, the update $\Delta\sigma = (C_{\text{BS}} - C_{\text{mkt}})/\nu$ is evaluated by a dedicated 33-cycle divider (\texttt{u\_nr\_divider}), concurrently with the 33-cycle Gamma divider (\texttt{u\_gamma\_divider}). To prevent numerical oscillation and divergence as Vega $\nu \to 0$, the core incorporates hardware clamping: the NR step is bounded by $|\Delta\sigma| \le 0.25$ (\texttt{MAX\_STEP\_Q24}), volatility is clamped to $[0.01, 5.00]$, the seeder output is clamped to $[0.05, 3.00]$, and \texttt{u\_nr\_divider} saturates safely upon zero denominator. Updated $\sigma_{n+1} = \sigma_n - \Delta\sigma$ loops back with priority via an active TID scoreboard. Because Greeks are registered and transferred to egress solely upon transaction convergence, contracts requiring a second pass re-evaluate $\Delta$, $\nu$, and $\Gamma$ at the refined $\sigma_{n+1}$ during Stages~3--4, ensuring all exported sensitivities strictly correspond to the final converged implied volatility.

\subsection{Stage 5: Multi-Core Arbitration \& 128-Bit Egress (2 Cycles)}
Upon convergence, results are packed into a 128-bit streaming word: $\text{Egress}[127:0] = \{ \text{TID}[5:0],\, \Gamma[25:0],\, \nu[31:0],\, \Delta[31:0],\, \sigma[31:0] \}$. $\Gamma$ is allocated 26 bits by truncating 6 redundant high-order sign bits from Q8.24 (maximum observed Gamma across all 10,000 synthetic and 1,382 SPX contracts was 0.2357, safely within the $[-2.0, +1.999]$ representation range with zero saturation), preserving full 24-bit fractional resolution while achieving single-flit packing. A 4-way arbiter drains output FIFOs with zero handshake stalls in 2 cycles. Because loopbacks complete asynchronously, retiring results are matched via the embedded 6-bit TID tag at the downstream network interface, completely avoiding the latency and resource penalty of a hardware reorder buffer.

\subsection{Pipeline Latency and Sustained Throughput Reconciliation}
Cold transaction latency from ingress to egress is $L_{\text{cold}} = 1\,(\text{Stg 1}) + 64\,(\text{Stg 2}) + 1\,(\text{Q}) + 126\,(\text{Stg 3}) + 33\,(\text{Stg 4}) + 2\,(\text{Stg 5}) = \mathbf{227\text{ cycles}}$. Loopback bypasses Stages 1--2, requiring Stages 3--4 ($126 + 33 = \mathbf{159\text{ cycles}}$), yielding a 386-cycle two-pass total:

Loopback does not stall the Stage-3 $II=1$ datapath; however, each loopback consumes one ingress scheduling opportunity. During the 159-cycle loopback pass, the core processes 158 subsequent new contracts, with only one ingress slot displaced by contract $k$'s re-entry.

\textbf{Liquid Trading ($S/K \in [0.85, 1.15]$):} 97.6\% of contracts converge in 1 pass and 2.4\% require 2 passes (1.024 slots/contract). Across 4 cores at 100~MHz, net sustained throughput is $T_{\text{net, liquid}} = \frac{400.00\text{ MOps/s}}{1 + 0.024} = \mathbf{390.62\text{ MOps/s}}$.

\textbf{Extended-Domain Throughput:} For contracts in the deep-wing regime ($|S/K - 1| > 0.15$), the 18-cycle CORDIC logarithm fallback engages and Vega diminishes ($\nu < 0.05$), requiring an empirical average of 2.100 passes per contract ($400.00 / 2.100 = \mathbf{190.48\text{ MOps/s}}$ sustained rate under 100\% wing load, or $200.00\text{ MOps/s}$ under a simplified 2.0-pass nominal lower bound). For the measured extended-domain evaluation suite (56.06\% deep-wing [5,606 contracts], 43.94\% liquid [4,394 contracts]), the combined scheduling cost is $\bar{P} = 0.4394(1.024) + 0.5606(2.100) = 1.627$ slots/contract, yielding \textbf{245.8~MOps/s blended net throughput} on Artix-7 and \textbf{614.4~MOps/s} on Alveo U50.

\subsection{Critical Timing Path Analysis}
On Artix-7 200T, timing closed at 100.00~MHz ($T_{\text{clk}} = 10.000\text{ ns}$) across 189,999 endpoints on the -3 speed grade at $T_j = 31.2\,^\circ\text{C}$. Vivado multi-corner STA simultaneously verifies the \textbf{Slow process corner} (setup/max delay: $WNS = +0.005\text{ ns}$, 0 fails) and \textbf{Fast process corner} (hold/min delay: $WHS = +0.058\text{ ns}$, 0 fails), demonstrating timing closure for the reported setup/hold corner analyses. Across multiple P\&R runs at 79.45\% LUT density, setup slack converges in $WNS \in [+0.002, +0.008]\text{ ns}$ (nominal critical path 9.777~ns: 3.775~ns logic, 6.002~ns routing over 22 levels at Core~1 divider \texttt{SLICE\_X48Y87}).

A frequency sweep confirms 100~MHz is optimal for this density: higher clock constraints fail at 150~MHz ($WNS = -4.849\text{ ns}$, 2,407 failing endpoints) and 200~MHz ($WNS = -6.515\text{ ns}$) due to 4-core routing congestion, whereas an isolated single core achieves $WNS = +0.658\text{ ns}$ at 100~MHz (empirical $F_{\max} \approx 106\text{ MHz}$).

On the AMD Alveo U50, 16nm FinFET technology scaling reduces the critical path delay from 9.777~ns down to 3.910~ns, enabling physical timing closure at 250.00~MHz ($T_{\text{clk}} = 4.000\text{ ns}$) with $WNS = +0.073\text{ ns}$ and $WHS = +0.004\text{ ns}$ across 182,201 endpoints (zero failing). Both Slow and Fast process corners are analyzed concurrently, with all endpoints passing. This signoff confirms an empirical maximum operating frequency of $F_{\max} = 254.65\text{ MHz}$ on -2 speed grade silicon. The reported frequencies represent nominal post-route timing closure on the stated implementation targets; additional board-level timing and environmental margin were not evaluated.

\subsection{Power and Thermal Budget}
Vivado post-route vectorless power analysis yields an estimated total power dissipation of 4.258~W (4.126~W dynamic, 0.132~W static) at junction temperature 31.2~$^\circ$C on Artix-7 200T, occupying only 17\% of a 25~W low-profile SmartNIC power budget. On the Alveo U50, the 4-core array at 250~MHz consumes an estimated 9.560~W total (7.214~W dynamic, 2.346~W static, $T_j = 30.0\,^\circ\text{C}$), delivering 1.00~GOps/s at an energy efficiency of 9.56~nJ/op (physical board-level shunt measurement is targeted for extended journal work).

\subsection{Illustrative First-Order Error-Budget Analysis}
The observed MAE of $1.57 \times 10^{-4}$ ($0.0157\%$ vol) is examined via a first-order perturbation budget ($\Delta\sigma \approx \Delta C / \nu$): (1)~\textit{Pad\'{e} Logarithm ($\Delta_{\ln}$)}: $|u| \le 0.15$ bounds error to $\le 3.6 \times 10^{-4}$, inducing $\Delta\sigma_{\text{Pad\'{e}}} \approx 4.5 \times 10^{-5}$ ($0.0045\%$ vol); (2)~\textit{Hyperbolic CORDIC ($e^{-rT}, e^{-d_1^2/2}$)}: 16-stage precision ($2^{-16}$) induces $\Delta C \approx 7.6 \times 10^{-6} K$, giving $\Delta\sigma_{\text{CORDIC}} \approx 2.8 \times 10^{-5}$ for $\nu \approx 0.35 K$; (3)~\textit{Horner CDF \& Quantization}: 5th-order Horner error ($|\epsilon| \le 7.5 \times 10^{-8}$) yields $\Delta\sigma_{\text{Horner}} \approx 1.2 \times 10^{-6}$ ($0.00012\%$), while Q8.24 roundoff contributes RMS uncertainty $\approx 2.5 \times 10^{-6}$; and (4)~\textit{Convergence Termination}: with mean $S=\$50, \bar{T}=0.8$ ($\bar{\nu} \approx \$17.35$), mean termination error $\overline{|\Delta C|} \approx \$0.0025$ yields $\Delta\sigma_{\text{stop}} \approx \frac{\$0.0025}{\$17.35} \approx 1.45 \times 10^{-4}$ ($0.0145\%$ vol). Root-sum-square yields $\approx 1.5 \times 10^{-4}$ ($0.015\%$ vol), providing an illustrative consistency check with the measured DPI-C MAE; this calculation is not a formal worst-case error bound.

\subsection{Deployment Considerations and Scaling Projection}
Deployment spans two tiers: (1)~\textit{Tier-1 Inline Filtering} ($<$25~W PCIe mezzanine), where our Artix-7 design consumes 4.258~W post-route estimated power (17\% of a 25~W budget) leaving BRAM resources available for potential MAC/DMA integration; and (2)~\textit{Tier-2 Centralized Co-Processing} (75--225~W datacenter accelerators). On AMD Alveo U50, the 4-core engine closed timing at 250.00~MHz ($WNS = +0.073\text{ ns}$), delivering 1.00~GOps/s at 9.560~W with strictly 0 BRAM and 0 URAM (12.13\% LUTs, 576 DSPs). \textbf{Architectural linear scaling projections} (not completed implementations) indicate that 32 parallel cores on U50 would consume 4,608 DSPs (77.4\%) for a \textbf{projected 8.00~GOps/s} within $\sim$65~W board power (scaling dynamic power linearly while retaining static overhead, under the 75~W PCIe ceiling), while 44 cores on VU9P project to \textbf{11.00~GOps/s}; both are linear DSP-count and dynamic-power scaling extrapolations from the 4-core measured result, and have not been synthesized or verified independently. Crucially, on-chip BRAM/URAM blocks remain available for external 100GbE FIFOs and order-book tracking.

\section{Conclusion}
% -------------------------------------------------------------------------
We presented a four-core FPGA acceleration engine for low-latency streaming option implied-volatility solving and concurrent Greeks ($\Delta, \nu, \Gamma$). Synergizing Brenner-Subrahmanyam guess seeding, Q8.24 scale normalization, and a zero-BRAM microarchitecture, the engine achieves timing signoff at 100~MHz on Artix-7 200T (400~MOps/s, 4.258~W) and 250~MHz on Alveo U50 (1.00~GOps/s, 9.560~W) with strictly 0 BRAM/URAM. DPI-C co-simulation confirms high accuracy ($MAE = 1.57 \times 10^{-4}$) with 99.9\% of contracts within 1.0 percentage point error and 0.91~$\mu$s cold latency, demonstrating 28.1$\times$ higher throughput and 297$\times$ higher estimated operations-per-watt under the stated baseline assumptions relative to a 16-thread AVX2 host CPU.
